Wael Shawky was born in Alexandria in 1971 and spent part of his childhood in Mecca, in Saudi Arabia, before returning to Egypt. He studied fine art at Alexandria University and then in the United States, at the University of Pennsylvania. History, religion and the way stories are told — and by whom — run through his work.

His best-known project, *Cabaret Crusades* (2010–2015), is a trilogy of films that tells the history of the Crusades from the Arab side, drawing on medieval Arab chroniclers and on Amin Maalouf's book *The Crusades Through Arab Eyes*. All the characters are marionettes: for the first film he used two-hundred-year-old puppets from a museum in Turin, and for the later ones he had figures made in ceramic and in Murano glass. The dialogue is sung and spoken in classical Arabic. In other works, such as *Al Araba Al Madfuna*, children speak adult voices, and real people move like puppets.

For the Egyptian Pavilion at the 2024 Venice Biennale he made *Drama 1882*, a film-opera about the Urabi revolt against foreign control and the riots in Alexandria that preceded the British bombardment of the city, with music and libretto written by Shawky himself. He also founded MASS Alexandria, an independent study programme for young artists.
