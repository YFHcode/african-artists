Sculpture is Africa's most celebrated art: the terracottas of Nok, the bronzes of Ife and [Benin](/art-forms/benin-bronzes), and centuries of carved masks and figures made for ritual, power and memory (see [African sculpture](/art-forms/african-sculpture) and [African masks](/art-forms/african-masks)). Modern African sculptors took that inheritance in new directions. [Mahmoud Mokhtar](/artists/mahmoud-mokhtar) gave Egypt a national monument in granite; [Adam Henein](/artists/adam-henein) carried ancient Egyptian calm into bronze; Ben Enwonwu, Felix Idubor and the [Oshogbo](/movements/oshogbo-school) carvers renewed Nigerian sculpture; the stone carvers of [Shona sculpture](/art-forms/shona-sculpture) in Zimbabwe and the [Makonde](/art-forms/makonde-art) carvers of Tanzania and Mozambique became famous worldwide.

Later sculptors made monumental and political work: [Ousmane Sow](/artists/ousmane-sow) and his giant figures, [Seyni Awa Camara](/artists/seni-awa-camara) and her clay spirits, [El Anatsui](/artists/el-anatsui) with his bottle-cap cloths, [Romuald Hazoumè](/artists/romuald-hazoume) with his jerrycan masks, [Wangechi Mutu](/artists/wangechi-mutu) with her bronzes at the Metropolitan Museum, and [Bruno Catalano](/artists/bruno-catalano) with his hollow travellers. These are Africa's sculptors with Wikipedia articles, most looked-up first.
