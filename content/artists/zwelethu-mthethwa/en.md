Zwelethu Mthethwa was born in Durban in 1960 and trained as a painter. In the 1990s he became known for large colour photographs of people in informal settlements and rural areas, posed in their own homes — walls papered with magazine pages and packaging — and later of workers. Against the black-and-white documentary tradition of apartheid photography, the portraits insisted on colour, dignity and the choices people made about how they were seen. His work entered major collections and was shown internationally.

In April 2013 Nokuphila Kumalo, aged 23, was beaten and kicked to death on a road in Woodstock, Cape Town. CCTV footage recorded the attack, and prosecutors placed Mthethwa's car at the scene. He denied the charge and did not testify. In March 2017, after a trial of nearly four years, the Western Cape High Court found him guilty of murder, and in June 2017 sentenced him to 18 years in prison. The judge said his reputation as an artist earned him no special treatment.

The case, and the campaigning of sex workers' rights groups who followed it, raised hard questions in the art world about how it treated a celebrated artist accused of killing a poor woman. See our list of [South African artists](/artists/country/south-africa).
