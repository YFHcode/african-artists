Senegal has been one of Africa's great art centres since independence. President Léopold Sédar Senghor, a poet, made art a national project: the [École de Dakar](/movements/ecole-de-dakar) of Papa Ibra Tall and Iba N'Diaye, the tapestry workshops of Thiès, and the 1966 World Festival of Negro Arts. In reaction, Issa Samb and the Laboratoire Agit'Art made experimental, political art in the streets of Dakar.

Senegal's sculptors are among the most famous on the continent: [Ousmane Sow](/artists/ousmane-sow), whose giant figures filled the Pont des Arts in Paris in 1999, and [Seyni Awa Camara](/artists/seni-awa-camara) of Bignona, whose clay mothers and spirits were shown at *Magiciens de la Terre*. Painters such as Viyé Diba, Kre M'Baye and Ndoye Douts, and photographers such as Omar Victor Diop, carry the scene forward, and the Dak'Art biennale brings the continent's artists to Dakar every two years.
