## A long road to Cape Town

Vladimir Tretchikoff was born in 1913 in Petropavlovsk, in what is now Kazakhstan. After the Russian revolution his family fled east, to Harbin in Manchuria, and as a young man he worked as an artist in Shanghai and then Singapore. The Second World War caught him in South-East Asia; he spent the war years in Java under Japanese occupation, separated from his wife and daughter, who had reached South Africa. In 1946 the family was reunited in Cape Town, where he would live for the rest of his life.

## Chinese Girl

Tretchikoff painted exotic women, flowers, animals and dramatic scenes in a smooth, glossy, intensely coloured style. In 1952 he painted *Chinese Girl*, a young woman in a golden robe with a blue-green face; the sitter is usually identified as Monika Sing-Lee, whom he met in Cape Town. Mass-produced prints of it hung in living rooms across the world, sold in department stores in Britain and elsewhere.

His exhibitions in South Africa, Britain and North America drew huge crowds; critics and museums ignored or derided him. The tension between popularity and critical scorn became part of his legend.

## Reassessment

In 2011 the Iziko South African National Gallery held a retrospective of his work, and in March 2013 the original *Chinese Girl* sold at Bonhams in London for £982,050 to the jeweller Laurence Graff, who displays it in the Cape winelands. Tretchikoff died in Cape Town in 2006.
