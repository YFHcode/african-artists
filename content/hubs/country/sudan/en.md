Sudan's art history reaches back to the kingdoms of Kush and Meroë, whose pyramids, temples and sculpture rivalled Egypt's, and to the painted churches of medieval Nubia. Modern Sudanese art took shape around independence in 1956, when artists trained in Khartoum and Britain founded the [Khartoum School](/movements/khartoum-school). [Ibrahim El-Salahi](/artists/ibrahim-el-salahi), Ahmed Shibrain and their colleagues, including the painter and filmmaker [Hussein Shariffe](/artists/hussein-shariffe), built a modern art from Arabic calligraphy, African forms and Islamic ornament. Kamala Ibrahim Ishaq later broke away to found the Crystalist group.

Many Sudanese artists have worked in exile: the printmaker Mohammad Omer Khalil in New York, Rashid Diab in Spain before returning to found an art centre in Khartoum, and [Ahmed Umar](/artists/ahmed-umar) in Norway. The list also includes photographers, from the pioneer Rashid Mahdi to Ala Kheir, and two people better known for other things: the wildlife cameraman [Hamza Yassin](/artists/hamza-yassin) and [Leni Riefenstahl](/artists/leni-riefenstahl), listed for her contested photographs of the Nuba. These are Sudan's artists, most looked-up first. See also [North Africa](/regions/north-africa).
