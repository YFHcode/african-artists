Anna Boghiguian was born in Cairo in 1946 to an Armenian-Egyptian family. She studied political science and economics at the American University in Cairo, then art and music at Concordia University in Montreal. Since then she has lived on the move, between Cairo, India, Canada and Europe, drawing and painting wherever she goes; travel itself became her method.

Her work begins with drawing: quick, expressive figures and city views in pen, watercolour and gouache, often with words written across them, collected in notebooks and artist's books. In recent years these drawings have grown into large installations that combine paintings, cut-out figures, maps, sails, salt and beeswax. They deal with big subjects — the history of cities such as Cairo and Istanbul, colonialism, the trade in cotton and salt, war and migration — through the lives of ordinary people.

She showed at Documenta 13 in Kassel in 2012, and her installation *The Salt Traders* (2015) was one of the most talked-about works at the 14th Istanbul Biennial. In 2015 she was also among the artists of the Armenian Pavilion at the Venice Biennale, which won the Golden Lion for best national participation. Solo exhibitions have followed at the Castello di Rivoli and other European museums.
