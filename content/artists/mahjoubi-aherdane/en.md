Mahjoubi Aherdane was born in 1921 in Oulmès, in the Middle Atlas, into an Amazigh family. He trained as an officer, served as a caïd under the French protectorate, and then joined the struggle for independence, becoming one of the leaders of the resistance and the Liberation Army in the 1950s. In 1957, with Abdelkrim al-Khatib, he founded the Mouvement Populaire, a party that spoke for rural and Amazigh Morocco, and he served as a minister, including of defence, under King Hassan II.

Painting was his other life. His first real contact with it came in Nice in 1947, through a friend whose brother was a painter, and he never stopped. His canvases are bright and dreamlike, full of figures, animals and signs, close to a flamboyant surrealism; they draw on Amazigh symbols and the landscapes of the Middle Atlas. He exhibited in Morocco and abroad, including in Versailles and London. He also wrote poetry in French — while, he said, thinking it in Tamazight — and published memoirs.

Aherdane died on 15 November 2020, aged 99. See our lists of [Moroccan painters](/artists/country/morocco/painters) and [Moroccan artists](/artists/country/morocco), and the [Casablanca School](/movements/casablanca-school), which also turned Amazigh signs into modern art.
