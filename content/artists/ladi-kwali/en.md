## A potter of Kwali

Ladi Kwali was born around 1925 in Kwali, a Gbari village in central Nigeria where pottery was women's work, taught within families. She learned from her aunt to build large water jars by hand, coiling and pinching the clay, decorating them with incised patterns of animals — lizards, crocodiles, scorpions, fish — and firing them in the open. Her jars were admired enough that the Emir of Abuja (today's Suleja) collected them.

## The Abuja Pottery Training Centre

In 1954 she joined the Pottery Training Centre that the British potter Michael Cardew had set up at Abuja, then a town north of the present capital. She was the first woman to work there. She learned to throw on the wheel, to use glazes and to fire stoneware in a kiln, while keeping her own forms and decoration. The combination produced some of the finest ceramics of the century.

## International fame

Exhibitions in London in the late 1950s and 1960s, and tours with Cardew in Europe and the United States, made her famous; her works entered museums such as the Victoria and Albert Museum. She was appointed MBE in 1963, and in 1977 Ahmadu Bello University in Zaria awarded her an honorary doctorate.

## Legacy

Ladi Kwali died in 1984. Her portrait is on the Nigerian 20 naira note, and a street and a conference hall in Abuja bear her name.
