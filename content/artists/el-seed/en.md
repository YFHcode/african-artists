eL Seed was born in Paris in 1981 to a Tunisian family from Gabès. He grew up speaking Tunisian Arabic at home but only learned to read and write standard Arabic as a teenager, and calligraphy became his way into the language. He took his name from *Le Cid*, the French play by Corneille. Starting out in the graffiti scene, he developed a style that blends the flowing letters of Arabic calligraphy with the scale and spray paint of street art: calligraffiti.

In 2012, after the Tunisian revolution, he painted a verse of the Qur'an about mutual respect and tolerance up the 57-metre minaret of the Jara Mosque in Gabès. In Cairo's Manshiyat Naser, home of the Zabbaleen, the Coptic community that collects and recycles much of the city's waste, he and his team painted *Perception* (2016) across about fifty buildings. The words, by the fourth-century bishop Athanasius of Alexandria — "Anyone who wants to see the sunlight clearly needs to wipe his eye first" — can only be read as a whole from one point on the nearby Muqattam hill.

His other projects have taken his calligraphy to walls, bridges and public spaces across Europe, the Middle East, Asia and the Americas. See our lists of [Tunisian artists](/artists/country/tunisia) and [Tunisian painters](/artists/country/tunisia/painters).
