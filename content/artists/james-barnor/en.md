A major retrospective at the Serpentine in London in 2021 brought his work to a wide new audience.

James Barnor was born in Accra in 1929 and, after an apprenticeship with a relative, opened his studio, Ever Young, in the Jamestown neighbourhood of Accra in the early 1950s. He made portraits of the city's people and, as a press photographer for the *Daily Graphic*, covered the political events that led to Ghana becoming the first sub-Saharan African country to win independence from colonial rule, in 1957.

In 1959 he moved to London, where he studied photography and worked for *Drum*, the influential South African magazine, photographing fashion and portraits of Black Londoners, models and visiting celebrities, many of them for its covers. His pictures are among the liveliest records of the African diaspora in 1960s Britain.

Around 1970 he returned to Ghana, where he set up the country's first colour processing laboratory and continued to work for decades. *James Barnor: Accra/London*, the 2021 retrospective at the Serpentine Galleries, drew on an archive of tens of thousands of images. Half a century after his *Drum* covers, [Misan Harriman](/artists/misan-harriman) became the first Black man to photograph a cover of *British Vogue*.
