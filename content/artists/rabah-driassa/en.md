Rabah Driassa was born in Blida, south-west of Algiers, in 1934. He began his career as a singer in 1953, after working in other trades, and over the following decades became one of the most popular voices in Algeria. Drawing on the *bedoui* song of the high plateaus and mixing it with other Algerian styles, he created a form of popular music that was easy to sing along to and rooted in the country's oral poetry.

His songs — *Nedjma qotbiya* ("The North Star"), *Yahya wlad bladi*, his version of the old love story *Hizia*, *El Goumri* and *Mabrouk alina* among them — were sung at weddings and family celebrations across the Maghreb, and after independence his patriotic songs became part of the national repertoire. He wrote and composed much of his own material.

Driassa was also a poet and a painter. His painting, much less known than his music, is the reason he appears in our lists of Algerian painters: Wikidata records him as a painter as well as a singer. He died at home in Blida on 8 October 2021, aged 87, and was mourned as one of the pillars of Algerian popular culture.
