Bruce Onobrakpeya was born in 1932 in Agbarha-Otor, in what is now Delta State, Nigeria, into an Urhobo family. He studied fine art at the Nigerian College of Arts, Science and Technology in Zaria, where in 1958 he joined fellow students, including Uche Okeke and Demas Nwoko, in the [Zaria Art Society](/movements/zaria-art-society), committed to a "natural synthesis" of Nigerian traditions and modern art.

He found his synthesis above all in printmaking. In the late 1960s he developed a deep etching technique he called the plastograph, and later metal-foil reliefs and other processes that let him build rich, textured surfaces. His images draw on Urhobo myths and proverbs, Christian themes and the life of Nigeria, in dense patterns of figures and signs.

In 1998 he began the Harmattan Workshop in Agbarha-Otor, an annual gathering where artists from across Nigeria and abroad learn, experiment and teach, run since 1999 by the Bruce Onobrakpeya Foundation.
