## From Soloba to Bamako

Malick Sidibé was born in 1935 in Soloba, a village in southern Mali. Noticed for his drawing, he was sent to study at a school of crafts in Bamako, then the capital of French Sudan. In the 1950s he went to work for a French photographer in the city, learned the trade, and in 1962 — two years after independence — opened his own studio, Studio Malick, in the Bagadadji neighbourhood.

## The nights

Bamako in the 1960s was full of young people who had grown up in a new country and wanted to look like it. They formed clubs, threw parties, danced to rock'n'roll, twist and Cuban music, and dressed with care. Sidibé went with them. On a good weekend he might cover several parties a night, then print the pictures and pin them up outside his studio for people to find themselves and order copies.

The photographs are joyful and intimate: couples dancing barefoot, friends showing off their clothes and records, swimmers on the banks of the Niger. Sidibé was trusted, and it shows. A 1963 picture of a young couple dancing, *Nuit de Noël (Happy Club)*, became one of his most reproduced images.

## The studio

By day, and increasingly in the 1970s as the party scene faded, Sidibé made portraits in the studio, against striped backdrops and patterned floors, of sitters who brought their own props and attitude. He also repaired cameras for much of his life.

## Recognition

His work reached international audiences in the 1990s — the decade in which the [Rencontres de Bamako](/guides/african-art-fairs-and-biennales), the African photography biennale, was founded in his city in 1994. Recognition followed: the Hasselblad Award in 2003 and, in 2007, the Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement at the Venice Biennale — the first given to a photographer. He died in Bamako in 2016.

## Related artists

Sidibé is inseparable from [Seydou Keïta](/artists/seydou-keita), the older Bamako studio photographer whose formal portraits of the 1950s capture the city a decade earlier. For music from the same West African dance floors, see [mbalax](/music/mbalax) and [highlife](/music/highlife).
