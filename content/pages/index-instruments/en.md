Africa has thousands of traditional instruments, and no short list can cover them. This guide begins with six that readers search for most, and that between them show the main families: strings, xylophones, drums and thumb pianos. Each page explains where the instrument comes from, how it is made and played, and who its best-known players are.

**Strings.** The [kora](/instruments/kora) is a 21-string harp-lute played by the griot families of the Mandinka, in The Gambia, Senegal, Mali and Guinea.

**Xylophones.** The [balafon](/instruments/balafon) is the gourd-resonated xylophone of the Mande peoples; the Sosso-Bala of Niagassola, in Guinea, is on UNESCO's list of intangible heritage.

**Drums.** The [djembe](/instruments/djembe) is the goblet drum of the Mande world that the national ballet of Guinea carried abroad; the [talking drum](/instruments/talking-drum) is an hourglass drum whose pitch follows the tones of speech.

**Thumb pianos.** The [mbira](/instruments/mbira) of the Shona of Zimbabwe is played to call the ancestors, and the [kalimba](/instruments/kalimba) is the modern instrument adapted from it.

Many of these instruments belong to **West Africa**, where hereditary musicians, the jeli or griots, have kept histories and praise songs for centuries; see [West Africa](/regions/west-africa) and, for Zimbabwe's mbira, [Southern Africa](/regions/southern-africa). For the popular music these instruments feed into, from highlife to mbalax, see our [music genres](/music).
