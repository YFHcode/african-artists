Kwame Akoto-Bamfo was born in Ghana in 1983. Drawing on the Akan tradition of funerary terracotta heads, which portray and honour the dead, he began the *Nkyinkyim* project — the name comes from an Adinkra symbol of twisting paths and life's journeys — to commemorate the millions of Africans taken in the transatlantic slave trade and to confront its legacy. The project has grown to thousands of sculpted heads, many modelled on living Ghanaians.

For the National Memorial for Peace and Justice in Montgomery, Alabama, opened in 2018 by the Equal Justice Initiative to remember the victims of lynching in the United States, he created a sculpture of enslaved men, women and children in chains, at the entrance to the memorial. He was refused a visa to travel to the United States, and the work was completed without him being there.

He has also made the *Blank Slate Monument*, which toured the United States in 2021, and works on restorative justice across the African diaspora.
