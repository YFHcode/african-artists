Mohamed Mrabet was born in 1936 (the date varies between sources) and grew up in and around Tangier, then an international zone full of foreign writers and artists. He never went to school and never learned to read or write. In the early 1960s he met Paul Bowles, the American novelist and composer who had settled in Tangier, and began to tell him stories.

Bowles recorded Mrabet's tales on tape — stories of hashish, love, trickery, violence and magic, drawn from his life and from the oral tradition of northern Morocco — and translated them into English. From *Love with a Few Hairs* (1967) and *The Lemon* (1969) to *M'Hashish* (1969) and beyond, they published more than a dozen books together until the early 1990s.

Mrabet is also a visual artist. He began making ink drawings in 1958, inspired in part by the Moroccan painter Ahmed Yacoubi, and later painted as well. His dense, swirling images of figures, animals and patterns sit somewhere between abstraction, Arabic calligraphy and the designs of henna, and he has exhibited them for decades, mostly in Tangier and Spain.
