Bruno Catalano was born in 1960 in Khouribga, the phosphate-mining town in central Morocco, the youngest of three children in a family of Sicilian origin. In 1970 the family moved to Marseille. He worked at sea and in other jobs in his youth before turning to sculpture, first modelling in clay and then casting in bronze.

His signature came from an accident. In 2004 a figure of Cyrano he was working on was flawed, and instead of starting again he cut away the damaged part, leaving a hole through the body. From then on he made the gap the centre of his work: men and women in coats and hats, carrying a suitcase or a bag, whose chests and legs are missing, so that you see the street or the sea through them. Catalano says they are about emigration and travel — the part of yourself you leave behind when you go, which stays connected to you all the same.

In 2013, when Marseille was European Capital of Culture, ten life-size *Voyageurs* were installed along the city's waterfront, and pictures of them spread across the internet. Since then his travellers have been shown in Venice, Paris, Asia and North America; *Le Grand Van Gogh* is on permanent display in Calgary.
