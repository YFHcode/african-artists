Tunisia sits on one of the oldest crossroads of art in the Mediterranean: Carthaginian and Roman cities, some of the finest Roman mosaics anywhere (many now in the Bardo Museum in Tunis), and the mosques, ceramics and textiles of Kairouan and the medinas. Modern Tunisian painting began with Yahia Turki, often called its father, who painted the streets and people of Tunis from the 1920s.

In 1949 a group of painters gathered as the École de Tunis; over the years its members included Yahia Turki, Ammar Farhat, Abdelaziz Gorgi, Hatem El Mekki and [Zoubeir Turki](/artists/zoubeir-turki), whose bronze Ibn Khaldoun stands on Avenue Habib Bourguiba. Later artists turned to abstraction and script: Néjib Belkhodja built cityscapes from geometry, and Nja Mahdaoui made Arabic letters into pure visual rhythm. Today the list includes Nadia Kaabi-Linke and the French-Tunisian calligraffiti artist [eL Seed](/artists/el-seed).
