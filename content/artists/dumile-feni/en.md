Dumile Feni was born in 1942 in Worcester, in the Western Cape, and grew up in the Eastern Cape and Johannesburg. Largely self-taught, he became known in the 1960s for monumental drawings in ballpoint pen and charcoal: figures with swollen limbs and twisted bodies, mothers and children, prisoners and the dead, that express the pain of the pass laws, forced removals and police violence.

His best-known work, *African Guernica* (1967), a huge charcoal drawing, takes its name from Picasso's painting of the bombing of Guernica and translates its horror to South Africa. Critics called him "the Goya of the townships".

In 1968 Feni left South Africa for London, and later moved to New York, where he continued to draw and sculpt. He died there in 1991, a year after Nelson Mandela's release, without having returned home.
