Bachir Yellès was born in Tlemcen, in western Algeria, in 1921. He went to secondary school at the Collège de Slane in Tlemcen, studied at the École des Beaux-Arts in Algiers from 1943 to 1947 and then at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris from 1947 to 1950. He was one of the very few Algerians of his generation to receive a full academic art training under colonial rule.

After that training he devoted much of his life to teaching. He directed the school of fine arts in Algiers for about two decades, until 1980, the years in which independent Algeria built its own art institutions, and many of the country's later painters passed through his hands. He also designed postage stamps for Algeria. His own painting — portraits, landscapes and scenes of Tlemcen and Algerian life — stayed close to the figurative tradition in which he had trained.

Yellès died in Algiers on 16 August 2022, aged 100. Algerian media called him the dean of the country's painters.
