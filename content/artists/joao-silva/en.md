## From Lisbon to Johannesburg

João Silva was born in Lisbon in 1966 and moved to South Africa with his family as a child. He began photographing the conflict in the townships around Johannesburg at the start of the 1990s, working with [Kevin Carter](/artists/kevin-carter), [Ken Oosterbroek](/artists/ken-oosterbroek) and [Greg Marinovich](/artists/greg-marinovich). Together they were known as the Bang-Bang Club.

## Sudan and Thokoza

In March 1993 Silva travelled with Carter to southern Sudan, where Carter took the photograph of a child and a vulture that won a Pulitzer Prize. On 18 April 1994 Silva was with the group in Thokoza when Oosterbroek was killed and Marinovich wounded. With Marinovich he later wrote *The Bang-Bang Club* (2000), which became a film in 2010. Our guide to [the Bang-Bang Club](/guides/bang-bang-club) tells the story.

## War photographer

After 1994 Silva covered conflicts far beyond South Africa, including Iraq and Afghanistan, and for many years worked as a contract photographer for *The New York Times*. He was known among colleagues for his calm under fire and for staying on stories long after the first wave of coverage had moved on.

## Kandahar, 2010

On 23 October 2010, while on patrol with American soldiers in Kandahar province, Afghanistan, Silva stepped on a landmine. He lost both legs below the knee and was badly injured. He kept photographing in the moments after the blast. After a long recovery in the United States, he walked again with prosthetic legs and returned to work as a photographer.
