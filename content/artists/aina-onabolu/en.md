Aina Onabolu was born in 1882 in Ijebu Ode, in south-western Nigeria. Largely self-taught at first, he began painting portraits in Lagos in the early 1900s, at a time when colonial officials doubted that Africans could practise art in the European manner. His careful, realistic portraits of the Lagos elite, such as *Mrs Spencer Savage* (1906), were a deliberate answer.

In the early 1920s he studied in London and Paris, and on his return he campaigned for art to be taught in Nigerian schools, teaching it himself. His efforts led to the appointment of the British art teacher Kenneth Murray in 1927, who trained a generation that included [Ben Enwonwu](/artists/ben-enwonwu). Onabolu taught for decades and died in 1963.

His insistence that Nigerians could master any visual language prepared the ground for the modernists who followed. See our lists of [Nigerian painters](/artists/country/nigeria/painters) and [Nigerian artists](/artists/country/nigeria).
