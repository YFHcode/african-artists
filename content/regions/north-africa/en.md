## Rock art and the green Sahara

Long before the desert, the Sahara was a land of lakes and grasslands, and its people painted and engraved its rocks. The Tassili n'Ajjer in Algeria and the Tadrart Acacus in Libya hold some of the world's great rock art: wild animals, cattle herders, then horses and camels as the climate dried. See [African rock art](/art-forms/african-rock-art).

## Egypt and Nubia

Ancient Egypt created one of the most influential art traditions in history, from the pyramids and temples to sculpture, wall painting, jewellery and the hieroglyphic script. To the south, in today's Sudan, the Nubian kingdoms of Kerma and Kush built their own temples and pyramids — more pyramids stand in Sudan than in Egypt — and Kushite kings ruled Egypt in the eighth and seventh centuries BCE. The Grand Egyptian Museum at Giza, which fully opened in November 2025, now presents the treasures of Tutankhamun together for the first time.

## Islamic art and architecture

From the seventh century, Islam shaped the region's art. Among its landmarks are the Great Mosque of Kairouan in Tunisia, founded in 670; Cairo's mosques and madrasas, including al-Azhar, founded in 970; and the al-Qarawiyyin mosque in Fez, founded in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri. Moroccan builders perfected **zellige**, mosaics of hand-cut glazed tiles in geometric patterns, together with carved stucco and painted cedar ceilings. Calligraphy, the art of beautiful writing, runs through all of it — and through the modern painters who reinvented it.

## Amazigh arts

The Amazigh (Berber) peoples of North Africa have their own visual language: geometric signs woven into carpets and tattooed on skin, the Tifinagh alphabet, and silver jewellery such as the large brooches that fasten women's clothing. Amazigh carpets — from the cream wool and dark lines of Beni Ourain rugs to the bright colours of Azilal — have become famous worldwide. Among the Tuareg of the Sahara, silversmiths make crosses and pendants in distinctive shapes. In the 1960s the artists of the [Casablanca School](/movements/casablanca-school) made these signs the basis of a modern art.

## Modern art

Egypt led the way. The sculptor Mahmoud Mokhtar's *Egypt's Awakening* (1928) became a national monument, and the painter [Mahmoud Saïd](/artists/mahmoud-said) gave Egyptian life a monumental modern form; later, Inji Efflatoun painted women prisoners from her own prison cell (see [African women artists](/guides/african-women-artists)). In Algeria, [Baya](/artists/baya) exhibited in Paris at sixteen. In Morocco, [Farid Belkahia](/artists/farid-belkahia) and the Casablanca School built an art from carpets, leather and henna; Chaïbia Talal became one of its best-known self-taught painters. In Sudan, [Ibrahim El-Salahi](/artists/ibrahim-el-salahi) and the [Khartoum School](/movements/khartoum-school) turned Arabic script into a modern visual language.

## Music

North Africa's music includes Algerian [raï](/music/rai), Moroccan [gnawa](/music/gnawa), the Andalusian classical traditions of the Maghreb and the great Egyptian song of the twentieth century.
