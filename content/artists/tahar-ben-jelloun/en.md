Tahar Ben Jelloun was born in Fez in 1944, went to school in Tangier and studied philosophy in Rabat before moving to France in 1971. His novels — *L'Enfant de sable* (1985) and its sequel *La Nuit sacrée*, which won the Prix Goncourt in 1987, among many others — made him one of the most widely read writers from the Maghreb. Most people who search for him are looking for the writer.

He has also painted for years, working with colour and light rather than figures: bright fields, signs and rhythms that recall the walls, doors and textiles of Morocco. In 2017 the Institut du monde arabe in Paris gave him carte blanche for an exhibition, *J'essaie de peindre la lumière du monde* ("I try to paint the light of the world"), with about fifty works on canvas and paper; it was the first exhibition of his painting in a French museum. In 2025 the Musée Mohammed VI of Modern and Contemporary Art in Rabat held a retrospective, *De l'écriture à la peinture* ("From writing to painting").

His novel *This Blinding Absence of Light* (2001) drew on the testimony of Aziz Binebine, a survivor of the Tazmamart prison and brother of the painter and novelist [Mahi Binebine](/artists/mahi-binebine).
