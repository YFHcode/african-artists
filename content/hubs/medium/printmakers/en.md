Printmaking has a special place in modern African art. Prints are cheap to make and easy to share, and in the twentieth century workshops across the continent used them to train artists and to tell stories. At the Rorke's Drift art centre in South Africa, the Namibian John Muafangejo made black-and-white linocuts of Bible stories, history and his own life that are among the best-loved African prints. In Nigeria, [Bruce Onobrakpeya](/artists/bruce-onobrakpeya) invented new ways of etching and relief printing, and the Mbari workshops at Osogbo spread printmaking to self-taught artists.

Today's printmakers range from [William Kentridge](/artists/william-kentridge), whose drawings and etchings sit alongside his films, and Diane Victor, known for her smoke drawings and etchings, to Rachid Koraïchi, who works with calligraphy and signs, Barthélémy Toguo of Cameroon, and the Sudanese printmaker Mohammad Omer Khalil in New York.
