Coert Steynberg was born in 1905 near Pretoria and trained as a sculptor. He worked in stone, marble, bronze, copper and wood, and from the 1930s received many public commissions. Around 1933 he made the statue of the Portuguese navigator Bartolomeu Dias for South Africa House, the country's embassy on Trafalgar Square in London.

Much of his public work commemorated the history of the Afrikaners as the National Party governments of the time told it: the Andries Pretorius monument in Graaff-Reinet, the Peace of Vereeniging monument and an ox-wagon memorial at Blood River. His best-known statue, of the Boer president Paul Kruger, stands at the Kruger Gate of the Kruger National Park. In 1939 he made the statue of the veterinary scientist Sir Arnold Theiler at Onderstepoort.

He also designed the springbok on the reverse of the Krugerrand, the gold coin first minted in 1967. In his later years his sculpture became increasingly abstract. He died in Pretoria in 1982. Such monuments are now read as part of the visual history of apartheid as well as of South African sculpture. See our list of [South African sculptors](/artists/country/south-africa/sculptors).
