## From mining to painting

Afewerk Tekle was born in 1932 in Ankober, the old capital of Shewa, in central Ethiopia. He grew up during the Italian occupation of 1936–41, and after liberation he was among the young Ethiopians the government sent abroad to train for the country's future. In 1947 he went to England to study mining engineering, but he soon persuaded the authorities to let him study art instead. He trained at the Central School of Arts and Crafts and then at the Slade School of Fine Art in London, and travelled in Europe to study church art and stained glass.

## Painting Ethiopia

He returned to Ethiopia in the mid-1950s determined to create a national modern art. He studied the country's own traditions — the icons and wall paintings of its churches, illuminated manuscripts, the processional crosses — and combined their colours and flat, rhythmic figures with the drawing and composition he had learned in Europe. His subjects were Ethiopian history and religion, its landscapes and people, and many portraits. *Meskel Flower* (1959), a young woman holding the yellow flowers of the Meskel festival, became one of the most reproduced images of modern Ethiopia.

## Africa Hall

His most famous work is in Africa Hall in Addis Ababa, the headquarters of the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa, inaugurated in 1961. For its main hall he designed three huge stained-glass windows, about 150 square metres in all, showing Africa's past suffering under colonialism, its struggle and its hopeful future. They remain among the largest and best-known works of public art in Africa.

## The laureate

Afewerk received the Haile Selassie I Prize for Fine Arts and many international honours, and he was a public figure in Ethiopia, known for his grand robes and his house-studio in Addis Ababa, Villa Alpha. He also designed stamps, murals, mosaics and costumes. He died in Addis Ababa in April 2012, aged 79. Later Ethiopian modernists, such as [Skunder Boghossian](/artists/skunder-boghossian), took a more abstract and experimental path, but Afewerk remained the country's most famous painter.
