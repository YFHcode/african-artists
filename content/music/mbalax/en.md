## From Cuban dance bands to Wolof drums

In the 1960s, the most popular bands in Dakar played Cuban music: son, cha-cha and pachanga, often sung in approximate Spanish. The Star Band, the city's leading group, was one of them. During the 1970s, musicians began bringing Senegal's own rhythms into this music — above all the **sabar**, the family of Wolof drums played at weddings, wrestling matches and street dances, and the **tama**, a small talking drum held under the arm.

The word *mbalax* itself comes from the name of a sabar accompaniment rhythm. As the drums moved to the front, the sound became unmistakably Senegalese.

## Youssou N'Dour

Youssou N'Dour was singing with the Star Band as a teenager. In 1979 he formed his own group, the Étoile de Dakar, later renamed the Super Étoile de Dakar, with which he became the leading figure of mbalax. His voice — high, piercing, rooted in the singing of Wolof griots — and the band's tight, fast drumming took the music to Europe and North America in the 1980s.

International fame peaked with "7 Seconds", his 1994 duet with Neneh Cherry, a hit across Europe. In Senegal he remained a national figure: musician, businessman and, for a time, a government minister.

## The sound

- Fast, crackling sabar and tama patterns that interlock with the drum kit.
- Electric guitars and keyboards playing short, repeated figures.
- Lead vocals in Wolof, often with praise singing, proverbs and social commentary.
- Dance breaks where the drums take over completely.

## Beyond Youssou

Omar Pene and his band Super Diamono, and the singer Thione Seck, were other giants of the genre. Later generations, including Viviane Chidid, brought mbalax into pop and R&B. The style remains the soundtrack of Senegalese weddings, nightlife and television.

## Related

Mbalax grew up in the Dakar shaped by the [École de Dakar](/movements/ecole-de-dakar) and its 1966 festival. Its fusion of local rhythm and Caribbean influence parallels [Congolese rumba](/music/congolese-rumba), and its political edge recalls [Afrobeat](/music/afrobeat).
