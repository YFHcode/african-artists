His portraits of film stars, artists and friends, and his self-portraits, are about memory, exile, desire and a vanishing Egyptian cinema.
---
Youssef Nabil was born in Cairo in 1972 and grew up watching the black-and-white melodramas of Egyptian cinema's golden age. As a young photographer he assisted the fashion photographers David LaChapelle in New York and Mario Testino in Paris, before developing his own technique.

He photographs in black and white and then colours each print by hand, as the studio retouchers and poster painters of Cairo once did. The result is soft, glowing and slightly unreal, closer to a film still than to a snapshot. He has photographed actors, singers and artists from Egypt and around the world, and made many self-portraits in which he appears, often with his face turned away, in landscapes and rooms far from Egypt.

He has also directed short films, including *You Never Left* (2010) and *I Saved My Belly Dancer* (2015). Nabil lives outside Egypt, and much of his work is about the distance between the country he left and the one he carries.
