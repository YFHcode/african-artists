# Yinka Shonibare

Expanded for batch 2, October 2026. Facts added by the expansion:

- Scramble for Africa (2003): fourteen headless figures at a long table; Berlin Conference 1884-85 (one review dates the work to 2000; 2003 kept, as the page already had): https://www.goyaandafrica.org/shonibare-scramble ; https://themodern.org/press_release/modern-art-museum-fort-worth-presents-focus-yinka-shonibare-mbe-january-13-march-24 ; https://brooklynrail.org/2004/01/artseen/looking-both-ways/
- Curator's note that the wax cloth is inspired by Indonesian batik, made in Europe, sold in West Africa: https://torch.si.edu/2009/11/postcolonial-provocateur-an-ironic-look-at-cultural-identity-at-african-art/
- Nelson's Ship in a Bottle: bottle 4.7 m long; outside the National Maritime Museum's Sammy Ofer Wing since 2012, free: https://rmg.co.uk/national-maritime-museum/attractions/nelsons-ship-bottle ; https://www.rmg.co.uk/national-maritime-museum/must-see/grab-selfie-nelsons-ship-bottle ; https://batch.artuk.org/discover/artworks/nelsons-ship-in-a-bottle-310258
- The British Library (2014): 6,328 second-hand books in wax-print fabric; 2,700 spines with gold-foil names (immigrants and opponents of immigration), rest blank; Brighton Museum and Art Gallery 2014 (HOUSE 2014, Brighton Festival), Turner Contemporary 2016, acquired by Tate 2019: https://www.artfund.org/supporting-museums/art-weve-helped-buy/artwork/13834/the-british-library ; https://www.tate.org.uk/press/press-releases/tate-acquires-installation-british-library-yinka-shonibare ; https://itsnicethat.com/news/yinka-shonibare-the-british-library-tate-modern-art-090419
- Wind Sculpture series: Wind Sculpture VII (2016), steel armature, hand-painted fibreglass resin, gold leaf, about 6 m, National Museum of African Art; Wind Sculpture VI in Ndubuisi Kanu Park, Ikeja, Lagos (British Council); Wind Sculpture (SG) I, Doris C. Freedman Plaza, Public Art Fund, 2018: https://africa.si.edu/collection/object/nmafa_2016-11-1 ; https://visualarts.britishcouncil.org/programmes/exhibition/yinka-shonibare-wind-sculpture-vi-2016/image/16783 ; https://news.artnet.com/exhibitions/yinka-shonibare-wind-sculpture-public-art-fund-1166135
- Not used: "first black British artist" on the Fourth Plinth (not checked beyond Wikipedia/Artlyst); the Pinnell Collection holding of Scramble for Africa (private collection, not displayed); Dutch wax sails of the ship (only "patterned textile" confirmed in the search; the page keeps its earlier wording).

Where to see the work: National Maritime Museum, Greenwich (free, outdoors); Tate (The British Library; check what is on display); National Museum of African Art (Wind Sculpture VII).
