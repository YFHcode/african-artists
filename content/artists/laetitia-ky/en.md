Laetitia Ky was born in 1996 in Abidjan. Inspired by photographs of the elaborate hairstyles worn by women in pre-colonial Africa, she began in her late teens to sculpt her own hair, using wire, thread and extensions, into forms that comment on the world: raised fists, a pair of hands covering a face, a woman carrying a child, words and slogans.

She photographs each sculpture, usually against a plain background and with a direct, theatrical pose, and shares the images online, where they have found an audience of millions. The work deals with women's rights, sexual violence, body image, racism and the beauty of natural Black hair, often with humour.

In 2022 she was one of the artists in the national pavilion of Côte d'Ivoire at the Venice Biennale, and she has since exhibited internationally and published a book of her work. See our list of [African photographers](/artists/medium/photographers).
