Mohamed Hamidi was born in Casablanca in 1941. He studied at the city's École des Beaux-Arts and then in Paris, and in 1967 returned to teach at the Casablanca school, where [Farid Belkahia](/artists/farid-belkahia), Mohamed Melehi and Mohamed Chabâa were reinventing art education around Moroccan crafts and signs. He taught there until 1975 and became one of the core members of what is now called the [Casablanca School](/movements/casablanca-school). In May 1969 he was among the painters who hung their work in the open air on the Jemaa el-Fna square in Marrakech, the exhibition often seen as the movement's founding act.

His painting combines flat, bright colour and hard-edged geometric forms with the human body: stylised, often erotic figures merged with signs drawn from carpets, tattoos and amulets. He also worked in mural and public art. Two of his works entered the collection of the Centre Pompidou in Paris.

Hamidi died in Casablanca on 6 October 2025, aged 84. See our lists of [Moroccan painters](/artists/country/morocco/painters) and [Moroccan artists](/artists/country/morocco), and the painter-novelist [Mahi Binebine](/artists/mahi-binebine).
