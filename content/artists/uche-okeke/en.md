Uche Okeke was born in 1933 to an Igbo family and studied fine art at the Nigerian College of Arts, Science and Technology in Zaria. In 1958, with fellow students, he founded the Zaria Art Society, sometimes called the "Zaria rebels", who rejected the colonial art curriculum. Okeke gave the group its guiding idea: a "natural synthesis" that would take what was valuable from Nigerian traditions and from the West to make an art for a newly independent nation. Read the story in [Zaria Art Society](/movements/zaria-art-society).

Okeke turned to uli, the flowing line drawings that Igbo women painted on bodies and walls, and to Igbo folklore, which he also collected and wrote about. From 1971 he taught at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, where he made uli the basis of a modern school of drawing and painting; among those associated with Nsukka were El Anatsui and Obiora Udechukwu.

He died in 2016. See also [Bruce Onobrakpeya](/artists/bruce-onobrakpeya), [El Anatsui](/artists/el-anatsui) and our list of [Nigerian painters](/artists/country/nigeria/painters).
