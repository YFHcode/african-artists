Ghana's photographers recorded the country's path to becoming, in 1957, the first sub-Saharan African country to win independence. James Barnor photographed the politics and the people of Accra from his Ever Young studio in the 1950s before shooting covers for *Drum* in London. Felicia Abban, often described as Ghana's first professional woman photographer, opened her studio in the 1950s and photographed the country's leaders and its people for decades. Philip Kwame Apagya made colourful studio portraits in front of painted backdrops.

Today, photographers of Ghanaian heritage such as Prince Gyasi, known for his intensely coloured images, and Campbell Addy, a fashion photographer based in London, have taken Ghanaian photography to a global audience. These are Ghana's photographers, most looked-up first. See also [kente cloth](/art-forms/kente-cloth) and [highlife](/music/highlife).
