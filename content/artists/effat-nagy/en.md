Effat Nagy was born in Alexandria in 1905 into a cultivated family; her elder brother Mohamed Naghi (1888–1956) was one of the founders of modern Egyptian painting. He encouraged her talent, and she went on to study art in Europe, including in Rome, at a time when very few Egyptian women could train as painters.

Her early work was figurative, but her mature art took a different path. With her husband Saad El-Khadem, an artist and researcher of Egyptian folk traditions, she studied popular beliefs, magic and the objects of everyday religion: amulets, talismans, zar ceremonies, ancient Egyptian and Coptic symbols. Her paintings of the 1950s and 1960s turned these into dense, mysterious compositions full of signs and figures, and she began to make assemblages that included pieces of antique painted wood and real amulets.

She exhibited in Egypt and abroad, and her work is now collected by museums such as Mathaf in Doha. After her death in 1994, the couple's house in Cairo became the Museum of Saad El-Khadem and Effat Nagy.
