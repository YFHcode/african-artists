Austin Stevens was born in Pretoria in 1950 and became fascinated by reptiles as a boy. He worked with snakes at a snake park and as a herpetologist, and became known for a record attempt in which he lived for 107 days in a glass enclosure with 36 venomous snakes, to raise money and attention for conservation; he was bitten by a cobra during the attempt and completed it.

He turned to wildlife photography and documentary television, travelling widely to find and photograph snakes and other animals in their habitats. *Austin Stevens: Snakemaster*, broadcast on Animal Planet from 2004 to 2009, made him internationally known, and he has written books about his expeditions. He later settled in Australia.

His work belongs to the wildlife side of South African photography, alongside the documentary tradition in our list of [South African photographers](/artists/country/south-africa/photographers). See also [African photographers](/artists/medium/photographers).
