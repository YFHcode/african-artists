Nigeria, Africa's most populous country, has a photographic history as varied as its regions. Studio photographers in Lagos, Ibadan and Kano made portraits for a growing urban public from the early twentieth century. J. D. 'Okhai Ojeikere spent decades photographing Nigerian women's hairstyles as sculpture, in one of the great documentary projects of the century. Samuel Fosso, who spent part of his childhood in Nigeria, made himself his own subject.

Today Lagos has one of the liveliest photography scenes on the continent, with the LagosPhoto festival, founded in 2010, and photographers working in fashion, documentary and art. Kano's Sani Maikatanga records the festivals and traditions of the north, and the Lagos-born Misan Harriman photographed a historic cover of British Vogue. These are Nigeria's photographers, most looked-up first. For the wider story, read [African photography](/art-forms/african-photography).
