Egyptian painting entered the modern age with a generation born around 1890: Mohamed Naghi, Ragheb Ayad, Youssef Kamel and, above all, the Alexandrian judge-turned-painter [Mahmoud Saïd](/artists/mahmoud-said), who gave Egyptian people and landscapes a monumental, sensual form. In 1939 the surrealists of the Art and Freedom group, among them Ramses Younan and Kamel El-Telmissany, rebelled against academic painting and nationalism alike.

After them came painters who looked at Egyptian society and its myths: [Inji Aflatoun](/artists/inji-aflatoun), who painted her fellow prisoners; Abdel Hadi Al Gazzar, with his visionary scenes of popular Cairo; Gazbia Sirry; the brothers Seif and Adham Wanly of Alexandria; [Effat Nagy](/artists/effat-nagy), with her folk amulets; and [Hussein Bicar](/artists/hussein-bicar), painter of Nubia and illustrator of millions of pages. Later painters include the abstract artist [Farouk Hosny](/artists/farouk-hosny) and, today, artists such as [Anna Boghiguian](/artists/anna-boghiguian). The list also includes [Farida of Egypt](/artists/farida-of-egypt). These are Egypt's painters, most looked-up first. See also [Egyptian artists](/artists/country/egypt).
