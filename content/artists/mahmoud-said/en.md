## Law and painting

Mahmoud Saïd was born in Alexandria in 1897 into one of Egypt's most prominent families: his father, Muhammad Saïd Pasha, served as prime minister, and his niece Safinaz Zulficar later became Queen Farida, wife of King Farouk.

He trained in the law and spent most of his working life in the legal profession, as a lawyer and then a judge. He painted throughout, studying with European artists in Alexandria and spending time in Paris, but only in 1947, in his fifties, did he leave the law to paint full-time.

## Egypt seen from inside

Saïd painted Egypt as a modern painter who knew its museums and its streets. His figures — peasant women, boatmen, dancers, workers at the shadoof, the water-lifting device of the Nile — are heavy, monumental and calm, with a solidity that recalls ancient Egyptian sculpture. Light is everything: warm, golden, often at dusk, as if the whole scene were cast in bronze.

He also painted portraits of Alexandrian society, religious scenes and landscapes of the Nile and the Mediterranean coast.

## Les Chadoufs

*Les Chadoufs* (1934) shows a veiled woman carrying water while men and a donkey draw water at a well. In April 2010 it sold at Christie's in Dubai for $2.43 million, against an estimate of $150,000 to $200,000. At the time it was the highest price paid at auction for a work by an Arab artist.

## Legacy

Saïd is counted among the founders of modern Egyptian painting, alongside the sculptor Mahmoud Mokhtar and others of the generation that came of age around the 1919 revolution. His family home in Alexandria is now a museum dedicated to his work.
