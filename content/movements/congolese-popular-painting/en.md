## Painters from the street

In the early 1970s, in the city then called Kinshasa, Zaire, a number of painters without academic training began making pictures for ordinary people. Several had worked as sign and billboard painters; they knew how to make an image readable at a glance and how to sell it.

Four names are usually given as the founders: [Chéri Samba](/artists/cheri-samba), Moké, Chéri Chérin and Pierre Bodo. Their pictures hung in homes, bars and barbershops, and in their own studios facing the street.

## What it shows

The subjects are the city itself. Bars and dance halls, couples and rivals, money troubles, police, church, sorcery, AIDS, corruption and the politics of Mobutu's Zaire — often treated with irony and humour, sometimes with anger. The paintings are literal and specific: they depict particular people and events and use painted text to name them, explain them or deliver a moral.

Each painter had a distinct voice. Moké recorded the social life of Kinshasa's outdoor bars and streets. Bodo, who became a Pentecostal Christian, painted surreal compositions that warn against sorcery. Samba's comic-strip captions turned his paintings into editorials.

## History in pictures

Tshibumba Kanda Matulu, from Lubumbashi in the south-east, painted the history of the Congo in a long series commissioned in the 1970s by the anthropologist Johannes Fabian: more than a hundred paintings covering pre-colonial times, colonial abuses, independence and the conflicts that followed. It is one of the most powerful visual histories of any African country.

## From Kinshasa to the world

International exhibitions from 1989 onwards — beginning with *Magiciens de la Terre* in Paris — brought the painters to museums and collectors abroad. Their success created a market, and with it copies and pastiches.

## Related

The Kinshasa painters belong to the same urban culture as [Congolese rumba](/music/congolese-rumba), whose stars and dance halls appear in their pictures. Compare them with the self-taught artists of Nigeria's [Oshogbo school](/movements/oshogbo-school), or with Tanzania's [Tingatinga](/art-forms/tingatinga) painting, another urban style born outside the academy.
