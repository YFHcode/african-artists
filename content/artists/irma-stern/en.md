## Berlin

Irma Stern was born in 1894 in Schweizer-Reneke, a small town in the Transvaal, to German-Jewish parents. The family spent long periods in Germany, and she studied art there. In Berlin she met the painter Max Pechstein of the Expressionist group Die Brücke, who encouraged her, and in 1919 she held her first exhibition at the Fritz Gurlitt gallery.

## Scandal and success in South Africa

Back in South Africa in 1920, Stern exhibited in Cape Town. Her strong colours, distorted forms and portraits of Black South Africans shocked conservative critics, one of whom called her work "Ugliness as a cult". She persisted, travelled widely and painted constantly, and by the 1940s she was established as the country's foremost modern painter.

## Zanzibar and the Congo

Stern's journeys produced her most celebrated works. She went to Zanzibar in 1939 and 1945, and to the Belgian Congo in 1942 and 1946, painting portraits of the people she met, often in richly carved frames made from Zanzibari doors. Her paintings of this period — *Arab Priest*, sold in 2011 for three million pounds, among them — are among the most valuable South African works ever sold. Today their exoticising view of Africans is also discussed critically.

## The Irma Stern Museum

She lived for nearly forty years in a house called The Firs in Rosebank, Cape Town, filled with her collection of African, Asian and European art. After her death in 1966 it became the Irma Stern Museum, run by the University of Cape Town.
