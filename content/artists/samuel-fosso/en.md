Samuel Fosso was born in 1962 in Kumba, Cameroon, and spent part of his childhood in Nigeria, which his family fled during the Biafran war. He moved to Bangui in the Central African Republic, apprenticed with a photographer and in 1975, aged about thirteen, opened his own studio there.

At the end of the day, he used his leftover film to photograph himself — in flared trousers, platform shoes and shirts he had made, posing like the pop stars on the record covers of the 1970s — and sent the pictures to his family. Those teenage self-portraits, discovered by curators in the 1990s, made his name.

He has continued to turn the camera on himself ever since, now in elaborate, staged series. In *Tati* (1997) he became a series of types, from a golfer to a "liberated American woman". In *African Spirits* (2008) he restaged famous portraits of the heroes of Black liberation and African independence — among them Malcolm X, Angela Davis, Patrice Lumumba and Kwame Nkrumah — with himself in every role. He is one of the major figures in the history of [African photography](/art-forms/african-photography).
