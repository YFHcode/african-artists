Painting in Africa is far older than the easel: the rock paintings of the Sahara and southern Africa go back thousands of years, and Ethiopian churches, Ndebele houses and Mauritanian walls carry painting traditions of their own. Modern easel painting arrived in the colonial period and was quickly made African. Pioneers such as [Mahmoud Saïd](/artists/mahmoud-said) in Egypt, Aina Onabolu and [Ben Enwonwu](/artists/ben-enwonwu) in Nigeria, and Irma Stern and Gerard Sekoto in South Africa opened the way.

Around independence, movements across the continent asked what a modern African painting should be: the [Zaria Art Society](/movements/zaria-art-society) and the [Oshogbo school](/movements/oshogbo-school) in Nigeria, the [Khartoum School](/movements/khartoum-school) of [Ibrahim El-Salahi](/artists/ibrahim-el-salahi), the [Casablanca School](/movements/casablanca-school), the [École de Dakar](/movements/ecole-de-dakar). Self-taught painters built their own traditions, from Kinshasa's [popular painting](/movements/congolese-popular-painting) of [Chéri Samba](/artists/cheri-samba) to Tanzania's [Tingatinga](/art-forms/tingatinga). Today painters such as [Njideka Akunyili Crosby](/artists/njideka-akunyili-crosby), [Marlene Dumas](/artists/marlene-dumas) and [Amoako Boafo](/artists/amoako-boafo) are among the most sought-after in the world. These are Africa's painters with Wikipedia articles, most looked-up first.
