## Cairo and Nice

Ghada Amer was born in Cairo in 1963. When she was eleven, her family moved to France, and she grew up in Nice. She studied art at the Villa Arson school in Nice, finishing in 1989, and at the Institut des hautes études en arts plastiques in Paris. She has said that as a student she was told painting was a male domain; her response was to paint with a needle.

## Painting with thread

From the early 1990s Amer began to "paint" with embroidery. On canvases covered with fields of acrylic colour she stitches outline drawings of women, often traced from pornographic magazines, repeating them across the surface. She leaves the ends of the threads long and loose, and they fall over the figures like tangled hair or brushstrokes, so that the images appear and disappear depending on where you stand.

The choice is pointed. Embroidery is a craft long taught to women at home, associated with patience and modesty; Amer uses it to show women's bodies and pleasure, on women's own terms. Other works stitch quotations — definitions of love, fairy tales, words from the Qur'an or from feminist writers — into the canvas.

## Sculptures and gardens

Amer also works outside the canvas. She makes sculptures, including cut-out steel and bronze letters and figures, and gardens: planted works in which flowers or grass spell out words about love, women or war. With her long-time collaborator Reza Farkhondeh she has produced paintings and prints together.

At the 1999 Venice Biennale she received a UNESCO Prize, and her work has since been shown in major museums in the United States, Europe and the Arab world. She lives and works in New York.
