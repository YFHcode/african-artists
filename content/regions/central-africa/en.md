## Kingdoms and their arts

Central Africa was home to powerful kingdoms whose courts commissioned art. The **Kongo** kingdom, on the lower Congo River, made contact with Portugal in the 1480s, and its rulers adopted Christianity; Kongo carvers and metalworkers made crucifixes alongside power figures. The **Kuba** kingdom of the Kasai region became famous for raffia textiles, carved cups and boxes, and royal masks. The **Luba** kingdom in the south-east created royal stools, staffs and *lukasa* memory boards. In the Grassfields of Cameroon, the **Bamum** and **Bamileke** kingdoms made beaded thrones and masks; King Njoya of Bamum (who ruled from the 1880s to 1931) even invented a script for his language.

## Sculpture

Many of the most famous works of African sculpture come from the region. **Kongo** *nkisi nkondi* figures, studded with nails and blades, were used to seal oaths and resolve disputes. The **Fang** of Gabon, Cameroon and Equatorial Guinea carved guardian figures for reliquaries holding ancestors' bones, and the **Kota** of Gabon covered theirs in sheets of brass and copper. The **Songye** made power figures and striated *kifwebe* masks. See [African sculpture](/art-forms/african-sculpture).

## Masks

The region's masks include the white-faced *okuyi* of the Punu of Gabon, danced on stilts; the *mwana pwo* of the Chokwe of Angola and the DR Congo, an idealised young woman; Kuba royal masks; and the beaded elephant masks of the Cameroon Grassfields. See [African masks](/art-forms/african-masks).

## Cloth and beads

Kuba raffia cloth, woven by men and embroidered and cut into velvet-like pile by women, is one of the great textile traditions of the world; raffia cloth once circulated as money in the Kongo kingdom. See [African textiles](/art-forms/african-textiles) and [African beadwork](/art-forms/african-beadwork).

## Modern art

In 1946 the French painter Pierre Romain-Desfossés founded a workshop known as Le Hangar in Élisabethville, today's Lubumbashi, and in 1951 Pierre Lods founded the Poto-Poto school of painting in Brazzaville — the same Lods who later helped found the [École de Dakar](/movements/ecole-de-dakar). From the 1970s, Kinshasa's [popular painters](/movements/congolese-popular-painting), led by [Chéri Samba](/artists/cheri-samba), painted the life and politics of the city. Bodys Isek Kingelez (1948–2015) built his visionary cities in paper and card, and today artists such as the photographer Sammy Baloji, from Lubumbashi, work with the region's colonial and mining history. Lubumbashi has hosted a biennale since 2008.

## Music

Central Africa's great gift to the continent's dance floors is [Congolese rumba](/music/congolese-rumba), born in Kinshasa and Brazzaville and recognised by UNESCO in 2021. Angola's [semba](/music/semba), the ancestor of kizomba, is included here because Angola sits with Central Africa in this guide.

## Restitution

Belgium's AfricaMuseum near Brussels holds one of the largest collections from the region, and since 2018 has rethought how it presents its colonial origins. See [where to see African art](/guides/where-to-see-african-art).
