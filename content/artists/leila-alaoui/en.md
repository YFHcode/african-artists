Leila Alaoui was born in Paris in 1982 to a Moroccan father and a French mother, grew up in Marrakech and studied photography in New York. Much of her work was about migration, identity and belonging: she filmed and photographed migrants trying to reach Europe, displaced Syrians in Lebanon, and people living between countries.

Her best-known series, *Les Marocains*, was made with a mobile studio she set up in towns, villages and markets across Morocco. Against a plain black background she photographed people in their own clothes and jewellery, then printed them life-size: a response to the exotic, orientalist images of Morocco made by Western photographers, and a portrait of the country's diversity in its own terms.

In January 2016 she was in Ouagadougou to photograph women's rights for Amnesty International. On 15 January gunmen attacked a café and hotel in the city; Alaoui was badly wounded and died three days later, aged 33. A foundation in her name preserves her work.
