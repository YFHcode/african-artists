## A childhood and a discovery

Baya was born Fatma Haddad in 1931 in Bordj El Kiffan, east of Algiers. She lost both parents young and as a girl went to work in the household of Marguerite Caminat Benhoura, a Frenchwoman in Algiers who noticed her modelling clay figures and painting, and gave her materials and space.

In 1947 the Paris dealer Aimé Maeght saw her work and gave her a solo exhibition at his gallery. André Breton, the leader of Surrealism, wrote the catalogue preface. Baya had just turned sixteen.

## Vallauris

In 1948 she worked at the Madoura pottery in Vallauris, in the south of France, where Pablo Picasso was making ceramics at the same time. The two met there, and many writers have traced an exchange between Baya's work and Picasso's paintings of Algerian women from the following years.

## Women, birds and gardens

Baya's world is one of women in long patterned dresses, surrounded by birds, fish, butterflies, plants, fruit and musical instruments — lutes, darboukas — in saturated blues, reds, pinks and greens. There is no perspective and no shadow. Figures and ornament share the same flat, rhythmic surface, and men are almost entirely absent.

Critics in the 1940s called her a "naive" or Surrealist painter. She accepted neither label. Today the work is read as an independent, deeply Algerian vision: an imagined interior world painted with total confidence.

## Silence and return

In 1953 Baya married the musician El Hadj Mahfoud Mahieddine and largely stopped painting for about a decade, raising a family in Blida during the Algerian War of Independence. She returned to painting in the 1960s, after independence, and exhibited in Algeria and abroad until her death in Blida in 1998.

## Rediscovery

Recent exhibitions have brought her work back into view, including *Baya: Woman of Algiers* at New York University's Grey Art Gallery in 2018 and her inclusion in the main exhibition of the 2022 Venice Biennale, *The Milk of Dreams*.

## Related artists and music

Baya belongs with other North African modernists such as [Farid Belkahia](/artists/farid-belkahia) and the Egyptian [Mahmoud Saïd](/artists/mahmoud-said). The instruments in her paintings belong to the musical world of Algeria, which also gave the world [raï](/music/rai).
