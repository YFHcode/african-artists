Pottery is one of Africa's oldest arts — some of the world's earliest ceramics were made in the Sahara — and for most of its history it has been the work of women, building pots by hand and firing them in the open (see [African pottery](/art-forms/african-pottery)). Modern African ceramic artists grew out of that tradition. [Ladi Kwali](/artists/ladi-kwali), a Gwari potter from northern Nigeria, joined Michael Cardew's training centre in Abuja and became world-famous; her portrait is on the 20 naira note. [Magdalene Odundo](/artists/magdalene-odundo), who learned from the same women potters, builds burnished vessels now in more than fifty museums.

Others took clay in different directions: Reinata Sadimba of Mozambique models fantastic Makonde figures; Safia Farhat was a pioneer of ceramics and tapestry in Tunisia; [El Anatsui](/artists/el-anatsui) worked in fired clay before his famous metal hangings; and the Ethiopian-born Etiyé Dimma Poulsen makes slender figures in clay and bronze. These are Africa's ceramic artists with Wikipedia articles, most looked-up first. See also [African sculptors](/artists/medium/sculptors).
