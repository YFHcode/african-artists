Hussein Shariffe was born in 1934 into a prominent Sudanese family. He went to England to study, first history and then art, and trained at the Slade School of Fine Art in London. In 1957 he had his first solo exhibition at Victor Musgrave's Gallery One in London, a gallery known for showing young avant-garde artists, and he won recognition in British competitions for young painters.

Back in Sudan he taught at the College of Fine and Applied Art and the University of Khartoum, alongside the artists of what became known as the [Khartoum School](/movements/khartoum-school), led by [Ibrahim El-Salahi](/artists/ibrahim-el-salahi) and Ahmed Shibrain. His paintings, often richly coloured and dreamlike, combine figures, faces and landscape in a personal, lyrical style.

From 1973 he began a second career in film, making documentaries and cinematic essays about Sudan, its culture and its people, and he also wrote poetry. After the military coup of 1989 he joined the many Sudanese intellectuals who left the country, and lived in exile, mainly in Cairo, where he continued to paint and make films until his death in 2005.
