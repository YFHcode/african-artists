Sankoh is on this site only because Wikidata records his early work as a photographer; he is known for nothing in art. Born in 1937, he joined the Sierra Leone Army in 1956 and trained in its signals corps. In 1971, as a corporal, he was imprisoned for several years over a mutiny. After his release he made a living with a camera, as an itinerant photographer in the south and east of the country, photographing weddings, and as a television cameraman. It was on those travels that he met the young radicals with whom he would found the RUF.

In 1991 the RUF, backed by Charles Taylor in Liberia, invaded Sierra Leone. The war that followed killed tens of thousands of people and displaced millions; RUF fighters became notorious for cutting off civilians' hands and arms, and for abducting children as soldiers. A 1999 peace deal made Sankoh vice-president, but fighting resumed, and he was captured in Freetown in 2000. Indicted by the Special Court for Sierra Leone in 2003 for war crimes and crimes against humanity, he died of a stroke in custody that July, before he could be tried.

The images that matter from that war were made by the photojournalists who documented it. See our list of [African photographers](/artists/medium/photographers).
