Kofi Antubam was born in 1922 in the Gold Coast, as Ghana was then called, and studied at Achimota College, where art teaching drew on local traditions, and later in Britain. He became a teacher and one of the leading artists of the generation that saw Ghana become independent in 1957.

Nkrumah's government wanted the new nation's symbols to come from African, and especially Akan, traditions rather than from Britain. Antubam, appointed state artist, designed the chair of state, carved and covered in gold leaf, on which Nkrumah sat at the Republic Day celebrations in 1960, and worked on the regalia and decoration of state buildings, including panels for the National Assembly. His paintings, murals and reliefs drew on Akan symbols, Adinkra signs and Ghanaian life.

In 1963 he published *Ghana's Heritage of Culture*, a study of the country's traditions. He died in 1964, aged 42. See also [Amon Kotei](/artists/amon-kotei), [adinkra symbols](/art-forms/adinkra-symbols) and our list of [Ghanaian artists](/artists/country/ghana).
