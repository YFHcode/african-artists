## From Kuils River to Amsterdam

Marlene Dumas was born in Cape Town in 1953 and grew up on a wine farm at Kuils River, outside the city. She studied art at the University of Cape Town in the 1970s, under apartheid, and in 1976 left for the Netherlands on a scholarship to the Ateliers '63 in Haarlem. She has lived in Amsterdam ever since, but South Africa — its politics, its racial categories, its violence — has never left her work.

## Painting from photographs

Dumas almost never paints from life. She works from photographs: images from newspapers and magazines, film stills, pornography, art history and her own snapshots of friends and family. Painting quickly, with thin, wet layers of oil or ink that run and bleed, she turns them into something less certain: faces that seem to stare back, bodies that are at once vulnerable and powerful.

Among her best-known works are *The Painter* (1994), a portrait of her young daughter with paint-stained hands, and large series of heads and figures: children, models, the dead, political prisoners and terrorists, Christ, her mother. Her subjects are those of the news and of private life, and she refuses to keep them apart.

## Recognition

Dumas represented the Netherlands at the Venice Biennale in 1995. Her retrospective *Measuring Your Own Grave* was shown at the Museum of Contemporary Art in Los Angeles and the Museum of Modern Art in New York in 2008, and *The Image as Burden* at Tate Modern in London in 2015. In 2008 her painting *The Visitor* sold at auction for more than three million pounds, then a record for a living woman artist.
