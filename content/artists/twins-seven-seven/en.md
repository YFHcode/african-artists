## A name and a story

Taiwo Olaniyi Oyewale-Toyeje Oyelale Osuntoki, known as Twins Seven-Seven, was born in 1944 in Nigeria. He explained his name as a story he loved to tell: his mother had seven sets of twins, and he was the only child to survive. Twins have a special place in Yoruba belief, and he made that history part of his identity.

## Oshogbo

In 1963 he arrived at the Mbari Mbayo club in Osogbo as a dancer and musician. The workshops run there by the writer Ulli Beier and the artist Georgina Beier encouraged people with no formal art training to paint and draw, and Twins Seven-Seven quickly became the star of what came to be called the [Oshogbo school](/movements/oshogbo-school). His works — on board, cut plywood and paper, densely patterned and crowded with figures — show the spirit world of Yoruba stories, the bush full of ghosts and hybrid creatures that also inspired the novels of Amos Tutuola.

## Music and the world

He was also a musician and bandleader, and travelled widely, exhibiting and performing in Europe and the United States, where he lived for some years. His work is in museums around the world. In 2005 UNESCO named him an Artist for Peace. He died in Ibadan in 2011.
