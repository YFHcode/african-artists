## Ancient sculpture

West Africa holds some of the oldest sculpture on the continent south of the Sahara. The Nok culture of central Nigeria made terracotta figures in the first millennium BCE. At Igbo-Ukwu, bronzes of extraordinary detail were cast around the ninth and tenth centuries; at Ife, naturalistic heads between the twelfth and fifteenth; and the Kingdom of Benin produced the brass heads and plaques now known as the [Benin Bronzes](/art-forms/benin-bronzes). In Mali, the ancient city of Djenné-Djenno yielded terracotta figures. See [African sculpture](/art-forms/african-sculpture).

## Masks and masquerade

The region's mask traditions are among the most famous in the world: the Dogon *kanaga* and Bamana *chi wara* of Mali, the plank masks of the Bwa of Burkina Faso, Senufo, Dan and Baule masks in Côte d'Ivoire, and the Yoruba Gelede and Egungun masquerades of Nigeria and Benin. They are still danced at funerals, initiations and festivals. See [African masks](/art-forms/african-masks).

## Cloth

West Africa is a continent of cloth in miniature. Ghana gave the world [kente](/art-forms/kente-cloth), woven in narrow strips by Asante and Ewe weavers, and [adinkra](/art-forms/adinkra-symbols), stamped with symbols that carry proverbs. Mali is the home of [bogolan, or mudcloth](/art-forms/mudcloth). The Yoruba dye **adire** in indigo and weave **aso oke** for ceremonies. See [African textiles](/art-forms/african-textiles).

## Earth architecture and manuscripts

The Sahel developed a monumental architecture in sun-dried earth. The Great Mosque of Djenné, rebuilt in 1907, is the largest earth building in the world and is replastered by the whole town every year; the mosques of Timbuktu date back to the fourteenth century. Timbuktu was also a centre of scholarship, and its libraries preserved hundreds of thousands of manuscripts, many beautifully illuminated.

## Modern art

Independence brought new art schools and movements. In Nigeria, the [Zaria Art Society](/movements/zaria-art-society) argued for a "natural synthesis" of Nigerian traditions and modern art, while the [Oshogbo school](/movements/oshogbo-school) grew from open workshops; [Ben Enwonwu](/artists/ben-enwonwu) had already become the country's most famous artist, and [El Anatsui](/artists/el-anatsui) later made Nsukka a world centre. In Senegal, the [École de Dakar](/movements/ecole-de-dakar) gave the new state its painting and tapestry, and [Ousmane Sow](/artists/ousmane-sow) its great sculptor.

## Photography

Bamako's studios produced two of the most admired photographers of the twentieth century, [Seydou Keïta](/artists/seydou-keita) and [Malick Sidibé](/artists/malick-sidibe), and the city still hosts the African biennale of photography. See [African photography](/art-forms/african-photography).

## Contemporary scenes

Lagos, Dakar, Accra and Abidjan have busy contemporary art scenes, with events such as Dak'Art and ART X Lagos (see [African art fairs and biennales](/guides/african-art-fairs-and-biennales)) and new institutions such as the Museum of West African Art in Benin City.

## Music

West Africa's music — [highlife](/music/highlife), [Afrobeat](/music/afrobeat), [Afrobeats](/music/afrobeats), Senegal's [mbalax](/music/mbalax) and Cabo Verde's [morna](/music/morna) — is as influential as its visual art.
