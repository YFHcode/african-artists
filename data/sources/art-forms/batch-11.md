## kente-cloth ("What is kente cloth?", "What kente cloth means", stole paragraph)
- Word "kente": often traced to "kenten", a word for basket in Akan; also nwentoma "woven cloth" (en.wikipedia Kente https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kente ; adanwomase.com https://adanwomase.com/kente-history-2/ ; ghanaremembers.com). The page already carried the basket etymology; "Fante" there is a variety of Akan.
- Silk kente for royalty and the wealthy; designs worn by Asante royalty not to be worn by others (fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/kente/ ; adanwomase.com ; ghanaremembers.com). Sources are tourism and museum pages; consistent with each other.
- Stole paragraph: kente stoles at graduation and "most kente sold worldwide is printed" are the page's own existing statements.
- NOT used (not confirmed): that Asante weavers unravelled imported silk and rewove it; origin stories (contested).

## adinkra-symbols ("Adinkra cloth and fabric")
- Hand-stamped with calabash stamps and badie dye at Ntonso (existing page text). Factory-printed imitation is readily available (Cooper Hewitt https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2018/09/07/adinkra-message-and-medium/ ); some cloth in Ntonso is screen-printed with designs adapted from the stamps (en.wikipedia Adinkra symbols https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adinkra_symbols ); factory calico may be the base cloth, symbols still hand-stamped (Horniman Museum, objects HC.1999.1055 and HC.1999.314).
- "Repeats vary slightly" in hand stamping: a general observation, not a stated test; no source gave a definitive method of telling the two apart, so the page says only that the seller should say which it is.
- Page already says the Akan of Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire (summary): no new sentence.

## mudcloth ("What is mud cloth?")
- Bamana "bògòlanfini" = roughly "cloth made with mud" (momaa.org https://momaa.org/bogolanfini/ ; Leila Atelier https://leilaatelier.com/blogs/textile-stories/bogolanfini-mali-s-mud-cloth ; Wikipedia Bògòlanfini). Process as on the page (n'gallama leaf bath, fermented iron-rich mud).
- NOT used: dates of origin (sources disagree, 12th century to later), the 1970s revival (page's own 1978 Groupe Bogolan Kasobané statement stands as before).
