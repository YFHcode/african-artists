Algeria's art reaches back to the rock paintings of the Tassili n'Ajjer, among the greatest in the world, and includes Amazigh pottery, weaving and jewellery and the miniature painting revived in the twentieth century by Mohamed Racim. Under French rule, though, Algeria was mostly painted by Europeans, and Algerian artists had to fight for a place. [Baya](/artists/baya) exhibited in Paris at sixteen in 1947; [Bachir Yellès](/artists/bachir-yelles) became one of the first Algerians with a full academic training.

Around independence in 1962, [M'hamed Issiakhem](/artists/mhamed-issiakhem), who painted the grief of the war, and [Mohammed Khadda](/artists/mohammed-khadda), leader of the "painters of the sign", founded a modern Algerian painting. Later artists include Rachid Koraïchi, who works with calligraphy and signs, [Adel Abdessemed](/artists/adel-abdessemed), the photographer Mohamed Bourouissa — and the singer [Rabah Driassa](/artists/rabah-driassa), who also painted. This list brings together Algeria's artists with Wikipedia articles, most looked-up first. See also [Algerian painters](/artists/country/algeria/painters) and [North Africa](/regions/north-africa).
