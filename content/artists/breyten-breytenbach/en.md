Breyten Breytenbach was born in 1939 in Bonnievale, in the Western Cape, and studied fine art at the University of Cape Town before leaving South Africa in the early 1960s for Paris. There he married Yolande Ngo Thi Hoang Lien, a French woman of Vietnamese origin; under apartheid's laws the marriage was a crime in South Africa, and she was refused a visa to visit.

He made his name as a poet in Afrikaans, beginning with *Die ysterkoei moet sweet* (1964), and became the most celebrated Afrikaans writer of his generation — and, as an opponent of the regime his language was identified with, one of its most painful thorns. In 1975 he returned secretly to South Africa to recruit for Okhela, a clandestine anti-apartheid group, and was arrested and sentenced under the Terrorism Act. He spent seven years in prison, two of them in solitary confinement, before international pressure, including from the French president François Mitterrand, secured his release in 1982.

Painting ran alongside his writing throughout his life: figurative, dreamlike and often unsettling work, including many self-portraits, shown in galleries in Europe and South Africa. He became a French citizen and died in Paris on 24 November 2024. See our list of [South African painters](/artists/country/south-africa/painters).
