Mohamed Maradji was born in Casablanca in 1939. As a teenager in the 1950s he worked as a street photographer in his home city, taking portraits of passers-by for a dirham a picture. He became a press photographer in the years after Moroccan independence in 1956, and his career grew with the new state.

He has photographed the official and public life of Morocco under three kings — Mohammed V, Hassan II and Mohammed VI — including royal ceremonies, travels and visits by foreign leaders. His archive is a visual history of the Moroccan monarchy and of the country over more than half a century.

He has published books including *Les Trois monarques*, *Maradji : Témoin de son époque* (2010), a 400-page survey of fifty years of his work, and *Mohammed VI l'Africain*, about the king's travels on the continent. A very different Moroccan photography can be seen in the work of [Leila Alaoui](/artists/leila-alaoui) and [Hassan Hajjaj](/artists/hassan-hajjaj).
