Chéri Chérin was born Joseph Kinkonda in Kinshasa in 1955. He studied at the city's Académie des Beaux-Arts and, like other painters of his generation, worked above all for local buyers, painting pictures that hung in homes, bars and shops. From the 1970s he became one of the leading names of [Congolese popular painting](/movements/congolese-popular-painting), the movement of Kinshasa painters who told the story of their city in bright, readable images with written captions.

Chérin's work is political and satirical. He painted the decline of Mobutu's Zaire, the wars and the refugees, power, money and corruption, but also the dreams of ordinary Kinois — the bars, the music, the fashion, the hope of leaving for Europe. His compositions are crowded and theatrical, combining realistic detail with caricature, and his captions, often in French or Lingala, act like a newspaper editorial.

His paintings were shown in Europe and North America, including in the Fondation Cartier's exhibition of Congolese art, *Beauté Congo*, in Paris in 2015, and are in museum collections such as the Weltmuseum in Vienna. He also trained younger artists, among them JP Mika and Landry Mulala. He died in Kinshasa on 19 October 2025.
