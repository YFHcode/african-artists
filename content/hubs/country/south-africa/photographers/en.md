No country in Africa has produced more celebrated photographers than South Africa, and few photographers anywhere have worked under harder conditions. In the 1950s *Drum* magazine gave Black photographers a platform; under apartheid, Peter Magubane was jailed and banned for his pictures, Ernest Cole was forced into exile after *House of Bondage*, and Sam Nzima's photograph of Hector Pieterson in 1976 turned the Soweto uprising into a world story. David Goldblatt spent sixty years recording how apartheid was built into the landscape.

In the early 1990s Kevin Carter, Ken Oosterbroek, Greg Marinovich and João Silva — [the Bang-Bang Club](/guides/bang-bang-club) — covered the township violence that came before the 1994 election. Since then a new generation, led by Zanele Muholi, has turned South African photography towards identity, memory and the body. These are the country's photographers, most looked-up first.
