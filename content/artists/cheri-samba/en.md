## From billboards to canvas

Chéri Samba was born in 1956 in Kinto M'Vuila, in what is now the Democratic Republic of the Congo. As a teenager he moved to Kinshasa to work as a sign painter, painting shop signs and advertising. Around 1979 he turned to his own paintings full-time.

The training shaped everything that followed. His pictures are made to be read quickly from across a street: bold outlines, flat bright colour, and blocks of text that tell you what you are looking at and what to think about it.

## Painting that talks

Text is Samba's signature. Captions, speech balloons and long explanatory paragraphs in French and Lingala sit inside the picture, so a painting can be a joke, an editorial and a moral lesson at once. Subjects range from Kinshasa street life and relationships to AIDS prevention, corruption, migration and the global art market. He often paints himself into the scene, as witness, commentator or target.

## The Kinshasa popular painters

Samba is one of the founding figures of what is called [Congolese popular painting](/movements/congolese-popular-painting), a movement of largely self-taught Kinshasa artists — among them Moké, Chéri Chérin and Pierre Bodo — who from the 1970s painted the city's life, scandals and history for local buyers, in a style that owed more to advertising and comics than to art school.

## International career

Samba's international breakthrough came with *Magiciens de la Terre* at the Centre Pompidou in Paris in 1989. The Fondation Cartier in Paris became a long-term supporter: it invited him for a residency soon afterwards and in 2004 gave him a solo exhibition, *J'aime Chéri Samba*. His work is held by the Centre Pompidou and the Museum of Modern Art in New York, among other collections.

## Why he matters

Samba proved that a painter trained on the street could talk to the world without changing his language. His mix of image and text speaks to the same Kinshasa audiences as [Congolese rumba](/music/congolese-rumba), the city's other great popular art. Compare his public, outdoor ambitions with the monumental sculpture of [Ousmane Sow](/artists/ousmane-sow), exhibited on a Paris bridge in 1999.
