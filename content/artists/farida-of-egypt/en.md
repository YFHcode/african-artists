Safinaz Zulficar was born in Alexandria in 1921 into a family of the Egyptian elite; her mother's brother was the painter [Mahmoud Saïd](/artists/mahmoud-said), one of the founders of modern Egyptian art. In January 1938, aged sixteen, she married King Farouk and took the name Farida. The marriage ended in divorce in 1948.

After the divorce, and after the 1952 revolution ended the monarchy, Farida devoted herself to painting. She lived abroad until 1974, first in Lebanon and then in Europe, and painted Egyptian landscapes in an Impressionist manner before moving towards more visionary work shaped by Sufism, light and memory. She held exhibitions in Europe and the United States, and in May 1980, back in Egypt, an exhibition in Cairo.

Her art was long overshadowed by her royal biography, and scholars have only recently begun to look at it closely. Her paintings are in the collections of Mathaf: Arab Museum of Modern Art in Doha and the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Cairo.
