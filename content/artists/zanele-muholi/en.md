## Becoming a visual activist

Zanele Muholi grew up in Umlazi, a township south of Durban. They trained at the Market Photo Workshop in Johannesburg, the school founded by [David Goldblatt](/artists/david-goldblatt), and later studied in Toronto. From the beginning their photography was bound up with activism: South Africa's 1996 constitution was the first in the world to protect people from discrimination on the grounds of sexual orientation, yet Black lesbians and trans people were — and are — targets of violence, including so-called "corrective" rape and murder.

Muholi chose to call themself a visual activist rather than an artist. The aim, as they have described it, is to make sure that Black queer South Africans are seen, remembered and represented by themselves rather than by others.

## Faces and Phases

In 2006 Muholi began *Faces and Phases*, black-and-white portraits of Black lesbians and trans people, each photographed looking straight at the camera. The series has grown to hundreds of portraits taken over many years, sometimes of the same person at different times, and includes people who have since died, some of them victims of hate crimes. It is at once a celebration, a record and a memorial.

## Somnyama Ngonyama

In 2012 Muholi began *Somnyama Ngonyama*, a series of self-portraits. Using everyday materials — clothes pegs, scouring pads, rubber tyres, plastic — as costume and props, and darkening their skin in the image, Muholi addresses labour, racism, the history of the colonial photograph and the politics of how Black people are seen. The series has been shown around the world, including at the 2019 Venice Biennale.

## Recognition

Tate Modern in London presented a major survey of Muholi's work in 2020–21. Muholi also co-founded Inkanyiso, a platform for queer visual media. They are among the artists in our guides to [African women artists](/guides/african-women-artists) and [contemporary African art](/guides/contemporary-african-art).
