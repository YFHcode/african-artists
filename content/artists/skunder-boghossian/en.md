Skunder Boghossian was born in Addis Ababa in 1937; his father was of Armenian descent and his mother Ethiopian. After studying in London between 1955 and 1957, at St Martin's, the Central School and the Slade, he moved to Paris, where he spent nearly a decade. There he met the writers of Négritude, the surrealists and artists such as the Cuban painter Wifredo Lam, and developed his own visionary style: swirling, cosmic compositions in which forms from Ethiopian magic scrolls, church paintings and crosses meet African sculpture and surrealist imagery.

In 1966 he returned to Ethiopia and taught at the School of Fine Arts in Addis Ababa, where his experimental approach influenced a generation of students and set him apart from the more classical [Afewerk Tekle](/artists/afewerk-tekle). In 1969 he moved to the United States, and from the early 1970s he taught painting at Howard University in Washington, D.C., for some thirty years, becoming a central figure for African and African American artists there.

The Museum of Modern Art in New York had bought his work in 1965, the first purchase of a painting by a contemporary African artist, and his paintings are now in museums across the United States, Europe and Africa. He died in Washington in 2003. See [Ethiopian art](/art-forms/ethiopian-art) and our list of [Ethiopian artists](/artists/country/ethiopia).
