## Tangier

Yto Barrada was born in Paris in 1971 and grew up in Tangier, the Moroccan port that faces Spain across the Strait of Gibraltar. She studied history and political science at the Sorbonne, then photography at the International Center of Photography in New York, and went back to Tangier to work.

## The Strait Project

Her first major series, *A Life Full of Holes: The Strait Project* (1998–2004), looks at Tangier at a time when Europe was closing its borders. Spain lies only fourteen kilometres away, but for most Moroccans a visa had become almost impossible to get, and many young people risked the crossing in small boats. Barrada's photographs show the city as a waiting room: empty lots, people turned away from the camera, wallpaper of alpine landscapes, a boy looking out to sea. They are quiet, often oblique pictures about longing, departure and staying behind.

The series was published as a book in 2005 and established her internationally. In 2011 Deutsche Bank named her its Artist of the Year, with an exhibition, *Riffs*, at the Deutsche Guggenheim in Berlin.

## The Cinémathèque

In 2006 Barrada co-founded the Cinémathèque de Tanger in the old Cinéma Rif, an art deco cinema on the Grand Socco square. It became an arthouse cinema, a film archive and a meeting place for the city, and she has directed it ever since.

## Beyond photography

Her later work moves freely between film, sculpture, collage, prints and textiles. She draws on archives, botany, toys, natural history and Moroccan modernism, and in recent years has dyed fabrics with plants, a practice she continues at The Mothership, a residency and dye garden she founded near Tangier. Her work is in museums such as the Museum of Modern Art in New York, Tate and the Centre Pompidou, and in 2026 she represents France at the Venice Biennale.
