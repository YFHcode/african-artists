Riefenstahl made her name in the 1930s as a director for the Nazi regime. *Triumph of the Will*, her film of the 1934 Nazi Party rally, is one of the most notorious propaganda films ever made. After the war she was classified by a denazification tribunal as a "fellow traveller", and she spent the rest of her life denying that she had known of Nazi crimes.

In the 1960s she turned to Africa. With special permission from the Sudanese government, she made repeated journeys to the Nuba Mountains between 1962 and 1977 and photographed Nuba communities, their wrestling, body painting and ceremonies. *Die Nuba* (1973), published in English as *The Last of the Nuba*, was an international bestseller, followed by *Die Nuba von Kau* (1976). Sudan granted her honorary citizenship.

The photographs have been fiercely criticised. In her 1975 essay "Fascinating Fascism", Susan Sontag argued that they share the cult of the strong, beautiful body found in Riefenstahl's Nazi films; others have described a colonial gaze that froze living people into an "untouched" past. The books were long banned in Sudan, and a German–Sudanese research project now studies the images and their effects.
