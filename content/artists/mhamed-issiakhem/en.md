## The accident

M'hamed Issiakhem was born in 1928 in a village in Kabylia, and grew up in Relizane, in western Algeria. In 1943, during the Second World War, he was fifteen when a grenade taken from a military camp exploded in the family home. Two of his sisters and a nephew were killed. Issiakhem survived, but his left arm had to be amputated, and he spent long months in hospital.

The trauma marked his whole life and work. Pain, loss and the bodies of the wounded are everywhere in his painting.

## Algiers, Paris and Kateb Yacine

After the war he studied art in Algiers, including miniature painting with Omar Racim, and in 1951 he met the young writer Kateb Yacine, author of *Nedjma*. It was the start of a friendship that lasted until the painter's death: the two worked together on books, theatre posters and films, and were known as the "twins of *Nedjma*".

From 1953 to 1958 he studied at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris. During the war of independence he lived between France and Germany, and in 1962, the year Algeria became independent, he returned home.

## The painting

Issiakhem was an expressionist. His canvases are dark and heavily worked, built up with thick layers of paint that he scraped, scratched and reworked; figures emerge from them half-formed, with deep-set eyes. His great subject was the suffering of the Algerian people in war: mothers holding children, widows, the women who waited and grieved. He also painted haunting portraits.

Like his friend [Mohammed Khadda](/artists/mohammed-khadda), he wanted a modern Algerian art that was free of colonial exoticism; but where Khadda turned to abstraction and signs, Issiakhem stayed with the human figure.

## A national artist

In independent Algeria he taught at the school of fine arts in Algiers and took part in founding the national union of visual artists. From 1965 he designed the images of Algeria's banknotes and many of its stamps, so his work passed through the hands of millions. In 1980 he received the Simba d'or in Rome, a prize for African art supported by UNESCO.

He died of cancer in Algiers on 1 December 1985, aged 57.
