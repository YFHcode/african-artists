Zoubeir Turki was born in Tunis in 1924. He studied at the Zitouna and took classes in the city's fine arts school, and after taking part in nationalist activity against the French protectorate he left for Sweden, where he trained at the Royal Academy of Fine Arts in Stockholm in the 1950s.

Back in Tunisia he became one of the main figures of the École de Tunis, the group of painters who, around independence in 1956, set out to create a Tunisian modern art drawn from local life and traditions. Turki's speciality was the line: quick, witty ink drawings and paintings of the people of Tunis — musicians, craftsmen, coffee-house regulars, wedding scenes and the characters of the medina — that became some of the best-loved images of Tunisian life. He also painted large murals, among them works for the national radio in Tunis and in Monastir in 1962.

His best-known public work is the bronze statue of Ibn Khaldoun, the fourteenth-century historian born in Tunis, which stands on Avenue Habib Bourguiba. He was active in the national union of visual artists and served in the National Assembly from 1979. He died in Tunis in 2009.
