Safinaz Zulficar was born in Alexandria in 1921 into a family of the Egyptian elite; her mother's brother was the painter [Mahmoud Saïd](/artists/mahmoud-said), one of the founders of modern Egyptian art. In January 1938, aged sixteen, she married King Farouk and took the name Farida. The marriage ended in divorce in 1948, and Mathaf's biography says that after it she began to paint again, encouraged by her uncle.

After the divorce, and after the 1952 revolution ended the monarchy, Farida devoted herself to painting. Accounts of her years abroad differ: one has her leaving Egypt in 1963 for Lebanon, Switzerland and finally Paris, and returning in 1982; she held exhibitions in several countries, and Mathaf records a successful solo exhibition at the Méridien hotel in Cairo in 1980. Her pictures include landscapes and more abstract, visionary work.

Her art was long overshadowed by her royal biography; Mathaf says her recognition as an artist remained limited, both in Egypt and abroad. She died in Cairo on 16 October 1988 (one source says 17 October). A recent exhibition at the Liwan Gallery in Cairo showed her work with that of her granddaughter, Yasmine Perreten.

How to look at a Farida: set the life aside at first. The interest is in how a woman from a painter's family chose to paint after the court, and in the range of her media, from pastel portraits to mixed-media pieces. That reading is this site's own.

## Where to see the work

Mathaf: Arab Museum of Modern Art in Doha holds her work, including the mixed-media *Niqash* (about 1950) and a pastel *Portrait of Simon Sfen*; she is also listed in the collections of the Queen Farida Museum in Maadi, the Alexandria Museum of Fine Arts and the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Cairo. Ask each what is on view.
