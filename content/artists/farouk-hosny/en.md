## Alexandria

Farouk Hosny was born in Alexandria in 1938 and grew up in the city, by the sea that he later said shaped his sense of colour and space. He studied at the Faculty of Fine Arts of Alexandria University, graduating in 1964, and began to exhibit as a painter.

## Paris and Rome

Instead of teaching or living only from painting, he entered cultural administration. He worked at Egypt's cultural centre in Paris in the 1970s, a decade that put him in touch with European abstract art, and then directed the Egyptian Academy in Rome. In both cities he kept painting and showing his work.

## Minister of Culture

In 1987 President Hosni Mubarak appointed him Minister of Culture, a post he held until the revolution of 2011 — almost twenty-four years. He oversaw major projects, among them restorations of Islamic Cairo and of the Great Sphinx, the planning of the Grand Egyptian Museum, new museums and an annual programme of exhibitions and festivals. He appointed the sculptor [Adam Henein](/artists/adam-henein) to lead the design team restoring the Sphinx, and supported the Aswan sculpture symposium Henein founded.

His time in office was also controversial: critics accused the ministry of censorship, and a fire at a theatre in Beni Suef in 2005, which killed dozens of people, led to calls for his resignation. In 2009 he was Egypt's candidate to lead UNESCO and was seen as the favourite, but a remark he had made in parliament about burning Israeli books, for which he later apologised, turned many countries against him. He lost the final vote to Irina Bokova, 31 to 27.

## The painter

Hosny has always presented himself first as a painter. His canvases are abstract: broad fields of colour, gestures and textures, sometimes suggesting light, sea or landscape, often in strong reds, blues and earth tones. He has exhibited in Egypt, Europe, the Arab world and Asia, and his work is in museums in Egypt and abroad.
