## The township war

Greg Marinovich began photographing the violence in South Africa's townships at the end of the 1980s. From 1990, as fighting between supporters of the African National Congress and the Inkatha Freedom Party spread across the townships around Johannesburg, he worked there almost daily with [Kevin Carter](/artists/kevin-carter), [Ken Oosterbroek](/artists/ken-oosterbroek) and [João Silva](/artists/joao-silva).

## The Pulitzer Prize

In September 1990, in Soweto, Marinovich photographed a crowd of ANC supporters attacking and killing a man they accused of being an Inkatha spy. The sequence of pictures won the Pulitzer Prize for Spot News Photography in 1991. Marinovich has written and spoken openly about how the photographs troubled him: whether his presence changed what happened, and what it means to make a career out of another person's death.

## Thokoza

On 18 April 1994, in the township of Thokoza, Marinovich was shot and badly wounded in the same burst of fire that killed Oosterbroek. He was wounded several times in his career, in South Africa and in other conflicts.

## The Bang-Bang Club

With Silva, Marinovich wrote *The Bang-Bang Club* (2000), an account of the four photographers' years in the townships, of Carter's Sudan photograph and of the deaths of Oosterbroek and Carter in 1994. It was adapted as a film in 2010, with Ryan Phillippe playing Marinovich. Read our guide to [the Bang-Bang Club](/guides/bang-bang-club).

## Marikana

In August 2012 police shot dead 34 striking miners at Marikana, a platinum mine in North West province. Working as a journalist, Marinovich returned to the site, studied the evidence and reported that many of the miners had been killed away from the cameras, at a rocky outcrop known as Small Koppie. He expanded the work into the book *Murder at Small Koppie* (2016).
