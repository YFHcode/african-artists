## A visit to the museum

Adam Henein was born in Cairo in 1929 into a Coptic family; his father was a craftsman who worked in metal. When he was eight, a school trip to the Egyptian Museum showed him the statues of the pharaohs, and he decided to become a sculptor. He studied sculpture at the Faculty of Fine Arts in Cairo, graduating in 1953, and then spent time in Luxor and in Germany.

## Simplicity

From the start Henein looked for the essential. His sculptures of people, owls, cats, donkeys and birds are reduced to a few smooth volumes and clean outlines, with the stillness and weight of ancient Egyptian statues but without imitating them. He worked in clay, bronze and, above all, in granite, the stone of the pharaohs, and the pieces often seem to be listening or waiting.

He was also a painter. In Paris he developed a distinctive way of painting on papyrus with natural pigments and gum arabic, in muted earth colours, creating abstract and semi-abstract compositions.

## Paris and return

In 1971 Henein moved to Paris, where he lived for twenty-five years, exhibiting widely in Europe. He returned to Egypt in the 1990s. From 1989 to 1998 he led the design team for the restoration of the Great Sphinx at Giza, appointed by the Minister of Culture [Farouk Hosny](/artists/farouk-hosny); his eye as a sculptor helped the restorers understand how the monument had been carved.

In 1996 he founded the Aswan International Sculpture Symposium, which each year brings sculptors from around the world to carve the local granite, and which he led for more than twenty years. The works made there now form an open-air museum overlooking the Nile.

## Museum and legacy

In 2014 his home and studio in Harraniya, near Giza, opened as the Adam Henein Museum, with hundreds of his sculptures and paintings. He received Egypt's highest arts awards, and he died in Cairo in May 2020, aged 91. He is seen as the heir of [Mahmoud Mokhtar](/artists/mahmoud-mokhtar), who first tried to give ancient Egyptian sculpture a modern life.
