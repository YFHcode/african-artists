Dan Eldon was born in London in 1970 and grew up in Nairobi, Kenya, where his mother, Kathy Eldon, worked as a journalist. As a teenager he was already photographing, travelling across Africa and filling journals with collages of photographs, drawings, maps, tickets and writing.

In 1992 he went to Somalia, where famine and civil war were killing hundreds of thousands of people, and became one of the youngest photographers working for Reuters. His pictures helped bring the famine to the world's attention. On 12 July 1993, after a United Nations helicopter attack on a house in Mogadishu, Eldon and other journalists went to the site; a crowd turned on them, and Eldon and three colleagues were killed.

His journals were published in 1997 as *The Journey Is the Destination*, and a film of the same title was released in 2016. Kenya's own great photojournalist of the period was [Mohamed Amin](/artists/mohamed-amin). See our list of [African photographers](/artists/medium/photographers).
