South Africa has one of the richest art histories on the continent and one of its strongest contemporary scenes. Its oldest art is the rock painting of the San and their ancestors; the gold of Mapungubwe and the painted houses and beadwork of the Ndebele, carried to the world by Esther Mahlangu, belong to the same long story. In the twentieth century, painters such as Irma Stern, J. H. Pierneef, Gerard Sekoto, George Pemba and Ernest Mancoba made South African modernism, while art centres such as Polly Street and Rorke's Drift trained Black artists excluded from the art schools.

Under apartheid, artists such as Dumile Feni and the country's great photojournalists recorded what the system did to people. Since 1994 South African art has been shown everywhere: William Kentridge's animated charcoal drawings, Marlene Dumas's paintings, Zanele Muholi's photographs, Jane Alexander's and Mary Sibande's sculpture.
