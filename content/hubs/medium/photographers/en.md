Africa has been photographed by outsiders since the camera arrived on its coasts in the 1840s, but its own photographers have made some of the most important pictures in the history of the medium. In the studios of Bamako, Accra and Bangui, Seydou Keïta, Malick Sidibé, James Barnor and Samuel Fosso gave a newly independent continent its portrait. In South Africa, photographers such as Peter Magubane, Ernest Cole, David Goldblatt and Sam Nzima showed the world what apartheid was, often at great personal cost, and in the 1990s the photographers of [the Bang-Bang Club](/guides/bang-bang-club) covered the violent end of the system.

Today's African photographers range from visual activists such as Zanele Muholi to artists working with hair, costume and archives. This list brings together more than 270 photographers from across the continent who have articles on Wikipedia, most looked-up first; those with a profile on this site come first, as cards. For the history behind the names, read [African photography](/art-forms/african-photography).
