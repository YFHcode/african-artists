Adel Abdessemed was born in Constantine, in eastern Algeria, in 1971. He studied art in Batna and then at the École des Beaux-Arts in Algiers. In 1994, during Algeria's civil war, the school's director Ahmed Asselah was murdered with his son, and Abdessemed left the country for France, where he continued his studies in Lyon. He has since lived in Paris, New York and Berlin.

His work is often uncomfortable on purpose. He has made life-size figures of Christ out of razor wire, a boat filled with rubbish bags, and videos involving animals that led to protests and the closing of an exhibition in San Francisco. Violence, power and the images the media repeat are his recurring subjects.

His most famous work is *Coup de tête* (2012), a bronze more than five metres high of the moment in the 2006 World Cup final when Zinedine Zidane headbutted Marco Materazzi. First shown outside the Centre Pompidou during his retrospective there, it was bought by Qatar Museums and installed on the Doha Corniche in 2013, then removed within weeks after complaints, and later shown at Mathaf, the Arab Museum of Modern Art.
