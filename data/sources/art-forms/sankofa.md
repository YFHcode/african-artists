# art-forms/sankofa (16 Nov 2026)

One researched page as a test of depth over volume. English is the reference; the Portuguese edition adds nothing. The source pages could not be opened from the research environment (hosts not allowed), so facts come from search results quoting them; each is worded no more strongly than the result supports.

- Word: san (return) + kɔ (go) + fa (fetch/take); proverb "Se wo were fi na wosankofa a yenkyi", "It is not taboo to go back for what you forgot" (en.wikipedia Sankofa; adinkrasymbols.org; Brooklyn Museum 45.11.5, which spells it with Twi letters). Spellings vary: the page says so.
- Met Museum, Gold Weight: Bird (Sankofa), 1994.312.16, brass, 18th–19th century, Akan, Ghana: proverb rendered "There is nothing wrong with learning from hindsight"; motif "may be based on a mythical bird that flies forwards with its head turned backwards" or a bird reaching behind for food (https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/317688).
- Goldweights used to weigh gold dust, the Akan currency (Brooklyn Museum https://brooklynmuseum.org/opencollection/objects/57691); Science Museum Group holds an Asante sankofa weight.
- Egg "representing the future": one Brazilian source and common explanation; worded "often explained as".
- Film: Sankofa (1993), Haile Gerima; Mona/Shola, Cape Coast Castle; Berlin competition 1993; self-distributed via Mypheduh Films with Shirikiana Aina to independent theatres in Black communities (en.wikipedia Sankofa (film); rogerebert.com 2021 review; Hollywood Reporter 2021). 2021 restored release: from those 2021 reviews.
- Janet Jackson, The Velvet Rope (1997): symbol featured on the album (en.wikipedia Sankofa; the97.net). Not stated which form.
- African Burial Ground: rediscovered 1991 at 290 Broadway; 419 remains; in use 1690s–1790s; reburied October 2003 in hand-carved coffins from Ghana (NPS https://www.nps.gov/articles/afamburial.htm). Burial 101, heart-shaped design in iron tacks (Penn Press / NYT summaries). Memorial by Rodney Leon, completed 2007 (GSA); sankofa among its symbols (NPS memorial document; East Idaho News 2015).
- Seeman, WMQ 67 (Jan 2010) 101–22: not a sankofa, probably no African origin (History News Network summary of Sewell Chan, NYT, 26 Jan 2010, "Coffin's Emblem Defies Certainty"); NPS: "could be a sankofa symbol", "no one knows for sure"; Blakey unconvinced. "Hearts in British and American funeral decoration": Seeman's argument as summarised; Springate 2011 is cited in results with conflicting summaries and was NOT used.
- Brazil: Abdias Nascimento, "Sankofa nº 2 – Resgate (Adinkra Asante)", 1992 (UFC thesis 2025); Itaú Cultural, Ocupação Abdias Nascimento ("a concept that captures the essence of Abdias' practice: sankofa"); Elisa Larkin Nascimento, book Sankofa; Adinkra: Sabedoria em Símbolos Africanos, org. E. L. Nascimento and Luiz Carlos Gá; USP journal Sankofa (revistas.usp.br/sankofa). "Largest population of African descent outside Africa": common description, worded "often described as".
- NOT used: iron gates made by African blacksmiths in Brazil or the US (no source found); the heart as a "doubled bird" (one source); pronunciation stress.
- Drawings: public/images/symbols/sankofa-bird.svg and sankofa-heart.svg, drawn for the site (not traced from any image).
