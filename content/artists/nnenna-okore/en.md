Nnenna Okore was born in 1975 and grew up in Nsukka, in south-eastern Nigeria, where she studied painting at the University of Nigeria. One of her teachers was [El Anatsui](/artists/el-anatsui), whose transformation of found materials shaped her own approach. She later took a master's degree at the University of Iowa in the United States and went on to teach at North Park University in Chicago.

Her sculptures are made by hand, slowly and repetitively: burlap is unravelled, dyed, twisted and knotted; paper is torn and rolled; clay and wax are pressed into sheets. The resulting reliefs and hanging forms ripple across walls and spaces like skin, bark or eroding land. They draw on the textures of Nigerian markets and landscapes, and on processes of decay and regeneration in nature.

Her work has been shown internationally and is in museum collections. See also [Otobong Nkanga](/artists/otobong-nkanga) and our list of [Nigerian sculptors](/artists/country/nigeria/sculptors).
