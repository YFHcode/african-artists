Khadija Saye was born in London in 1992 to a Gambian family and grew up in the city. She studied photography and began making work about identity, faith and belonging, and about the Gambian traditions she grew up with.

Her series *Dwelling: in this space we breathe* uses the nineteenth-century wet-plate tintype process. In each self-portrait she holds an object linked to Gambian spiritual practice — amulets, a horn, prayer beads, incense — to her head or body. The small, dark, silvery images, made with a technique that is never entirely under control, speak of protection, inheritance and the search for something to hold on to.

The series was shown in the Diaspora Pavilion during the Venice Biennale in May 2017. On 14 June 2017, a fire destroyed Grenfell Tower, the tower block in west London where she lived; Saye and her mother, Mary Mendy, were among the 72 people who died. Her work has since been shown in Britain and abroad. See our list of [African photographers](/artists/medium/photographers).
