Kenya's art ranges from the beadwork of the Maasai and Samburu and the carved doors of the Swahili coast at Lamu to a lively modern scene in Nairobi. In the twentieth century, many Kenyan artists trained at Makerere in Kampala or abroad, and from the 1990s Nairobi's galleries, artist-run spaces such as the GoDown and Kuona Trust, and popular painters like Joseph Bertiers gave the city its own art world.

Kenya's best-known artists today work across the world: [Wangechi Mutu](/artists/wangechi-mutu), whose bronzes filled the facade of the Metropolitan Museum, and [Magdalene Odundo](/artists/magdalene-odundo), whose hand-built vessels are in fifty museums. Photography is especially strong, from [Mohamed Amin](/artists/mohamed-amin), whose 1984 pictures of the Ethiopian famine moved the world, to the activist [Boniface Mwangi](/artists/boniface-mwangi) and younger photographers such as Mimi Cherono Ng'ok and Jim Chuchu.
