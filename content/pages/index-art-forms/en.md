Most of the art made in Africa over the centuries was not painted on canvas or signed by a famous name. It was danced as masks at funerals and harvests, carved for shrines and royal courts, woven and dyed as cloth, threaded as beads, shaped as pots, and painted on the walls of rock shelters tens of thousands of years ago. Photography, arriving in the nineteenth century, became one of the continent's great modern arts.

This section explains each major art form — how it is made, what it meant, who made it and how to see or buy it — and the best-known named traditions, from Ghana's [kente](/art-forms/kente-cloth) and [adinkra](/art-forms/adinkra-symbols) to Mali's [mudcloth](/art-forms/mudcloth) and the [Benin Bronzes](/art-forms/benin-bronzes). For how they fit together over time, read our [short history of African art](/guides/african-art-history); to explore by place, see [African art by region](/regions).
