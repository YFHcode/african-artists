## Training abroad

Farid Belkahia was born in Marrakech in 1934. He studied at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris in the 1950s and then in Prague. Europe gave him technique; it also convinced him that Moroccan artists needed something other than European painting on canvas.

## Running the Casablanca school

In 1962, at twenty-eight, Belkahia became director of the École des Beaux-Arts in Casablanca, a post he held until 1974. In 1964 he brought in the painters Mohamed Melehi and Mohamed Chabâa and the art historians Toni Maraini and Bert Flint. Together they rebuilt the teaching around Morocco's own arts — carpets, jewellery, ceramics, painted wood, calligraphy and architecture — treating them as modern resources rather than folklore.

This is what became known as the [Casablanca School](/movements/casablanca-school). In 1969 Belkahia and his colleagues took their work out of galleries altogether with *Présence plastique*, open-air exhibitions on the Jemaa el-Fna square in Marrakech and a public square in Casablanca.

## Copper and leather

In the 1960s Belkahia worked with hammered copper. From the mid-1970s he turned to the material he is best known for: animal skin, treated with traditional techniques and stretched over shaped wooden supports — circles, arches, irregular forms — then coloured with henna and other natural dyes.

The surfaces carry signs: symbols drawn from Amazigh (Berber) writing and tattooing, Arabic letters, hands, eyes and bodies. Henna, which in Morocco marks weddings and celebrations on the skin, here marks a skin of another kind.

## Legacy

Belkahia showed that modern art could be made from the materials and signs of North Africa without nostalgia. Recent exhibitions of the Casablanca art school in Europe have brought his work and that of his colleagues to new audiences.

## Related artists and music

Belkahia's use of writing and symbol has a close parallel in the calligraphic work of [Ibrahim El-Salahi](/artists/ibrahim-el-salahi) and the [Khartoum School](/movements/khartoum-school). For another Maghreb modernist, see the Algerian painter [Baya](/artists/baya). For the sound of Marrakech's squares, read about [gnawa](/music/gnawa).
