## From the print shop

Mohammed Khadda was born in Mostaganem, on Algeria's western coast, in 1930. His family was poor, and as a boy he went to work in a printing house, where he learned typesetting and bookbinding. Handling lead letters every day gave him a lasting sense of the shapes of writing.

He taught himself to draw and paint, took evening classes, and spent his free time with a friend from Mostaganem, the painter Abdallah Benanteur. In 1953 the two young men left together for Paris.

## Paris

Khadda spent ten years in Paris, working at odd jobs, painting and meeting other artists and writers. He exhibited in Paris from the late 1950s. During those years his painting moved from figuration towards abstraction, and he looked for a modern language that would come from Algeria's own visual culture rather than from the orientalist images of the colonial period.

## Painting the sign

In 1963, after independence, he returned to Algeria. His mature work is built from signs: the curves and strokes of Arabic letters, which he freed from their meaning, alongside Amazigh motifs, the shapes of olive trees and the colours of the Algerian landscape. The poet Jean Sénac called him and the artists working in the same direction the *peintres du signe* — the "painters of the sign".

Khadda argued for this kind of abstraction at a time when the state wanted art to be realist and patriotic. He wrote essays on art, including *Éléments pour un art nouveau* (1972), helped found the national union of visual artists, painted murals and designed posters and book covers. He also made sculpture.

## Legacy

He died in Algiers in 1991. Today he is regarded, with his friend [M'hamed Issiakhem](/artists/mhamed-issiakhem), as one of the two great founders of modern Algerian painting; his work is in the national museums of Algeria and in collections across the Arab world, and in 2019 Google marked his birthday with a Doodle. Like the [Khartoum School](/movements/khartoum-school) in Sudan and the [Casablanca School](/movements/casablanca-school) in Morocco, he showed how Arabic script and local signs could become a modern art.
