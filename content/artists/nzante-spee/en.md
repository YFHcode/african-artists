Sunday Nzante Spee was born in 1953 in Mbem, in the grassfields of north-western Cameroon. He began painting young and, from 1976 to 1982, trained at art schools in Nigeria and Côte d'Ivoire, at a time when Cameroon had little formal art education of its own.

Back home he settled in Bamenda and founded the Spee Art Centre, a studio and training centre — possibly the first of its kind in the city. Living from painting was hard there, and he also painted shop fronts, signs and advertisements, decorated public buildings and played guitar in a reggae band. His studio became a school for younger painters, among them Angu Walters, who started out with him and became one of Cameroon's best-known contemporary artists.

Spee's own pictures are dense and dreamlike: figures, faces, animals and buildings melt into one another in bright colours, mixing the fragmented forms of cubism, the strangeness of surrealism and the humour of caricature, often with comments on Cameroonian society. He called this style the "melting age". His work was collected abroad, and he died in Stockton, California, in May 2005. See our list of [Cameroonian artists](/artists/country/cameroon).
