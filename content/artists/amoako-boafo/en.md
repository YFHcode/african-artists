## From Accra to Vienna

Amoako Boafo was born in Accra in 1984 and grew up in the Osu neighbourhood of the city. He studied at the Ghanatta College of Art and Design in Accra and in 2014 moved to Vienna, where he continued his training at the Academy of Fine Arts. In Austria he began to paint the Black community around him, partly in response to the racism he encountered and the invisibility of Black people in its museums.

## Painting with the fingers

Boafo paints his figures' clothes and backgrounds with a brush, in flat colours and patterns, but models their skin with his fingers, in swirling, sculptural strokes. The contrast gives his sitters a striking physical presence. His subjects are people of the Black diaspora, often friends and fellow artists, painted with dignity, style and directness.

## A rapid rise

In 2019 the American painter Kehinde Wiley encouraged collectors and dealers to look at his work, and his career took off. Within months his paintings, bought cheaply from the studio, were being resold at auction for many times their price — a speculative boom that he and others criticised, as it brought him little and threatened his career. He has since worked with major galleries and museums, and in 2020 collaborated with Kim Jones on a Dior menswear collection.

## dot.ateliers

In 2022 he opened dot.ateliers in Accra, a space with studios, a residency and an exhibition gallery for young artists, part of a generation of Ghanaian artists investing in institutions at home.
