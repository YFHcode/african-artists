## Ethiopia

Ethiopia has one of the longest continuous art traditions in Africa: the stelae of Aksum, the eleven rock-hewn churches of Lalibela, illuminated manuscripts, icons and processional crosses, and a modern school of painting founded in Addis Ababa in 1958. See [Ethiopian art](/art-forms/ethiopian-art).

## The Swahili coast

From Somalia to Mozambique, the Swahili towns of the Indian Ocean coast traded for centuries with Arabia, Persia and India. They built in coral stone and lime, and their carpenters carved massive wooden doors with elaborate frames and brass studs. The old towns of Lamu in Kenya and Zanzibar in Tanzania, and the ruins of Kilwa Kisiwani, are World Heritage Sites. The printed **kanga**, with its Swahili proverb, is the coast's signature cloth (see [African textiles](/art-forms/african-textiles)).

## Rock art

Painted shelters at Kondoa in Tanzania and the remarkably preserved paintings of cattle at Laas Geel in Somaliland belong to the great rock art of the continent. See [African rock art](/art-forms/african-rock-art).

## Beadwork, barkcloth and imigongo

Maasai and Samburu women in Kenya and Tanzania are famous for their beaded collars and ornaments (see [African beadwork](/art-forms/african-beadwork)). In Uganda, the Baganda beat the bark of the mutuba fig into barkcloth, recognised by UNESCO. In Rwanda, women make **imigongo**: geometric relief panels modelled from cow dung, painted in black, white and red.

## Tanzania: Makonde and Tingatinga

Tanzania is the home of two of East Africa's best-known modern art forms: the blackwood sculpture of the [Makonde](/art-forms/makonde-art), with its ujamaa "family trees" and shetani spirits, and [Tingatinga](/art-forms/tingatinga), the glossy enamel painting style created in Dar es Salaam around 1968.

## Makerere and modern art

In 1937 the British artist Margaret Trowell began teaching art at Makerere College in Kampala; the school that grew from her classes, now part of Makerere University, trained artists from across East Africa. In Kenya, artists' workshops and studios in Nairobi, such as the Kuona Trust, became hubs for a new generation.

## Contemporary artists

East African artists are prominent today: the Kenyan [Wangechi Mutu](/artists/wangechi-mutu), whose bronzes stood on the facade of the Metropolitan Museum; the Ethiopian-born painter Julie Mehretu; the Ethiopian artist Elias Sime, who builds works from recycled electronics; and the Kenyan painter Michael Armitage, who paints on Ugandan barkcloth. See [contemporary African art](/guides/contemporary-african-art).

## Music

Kenya's benga, Tanzania's taarab and bongo flava, and the Ethiopian jazz made famous by Mulatu Astatke are among East Africa's great musical exports.
