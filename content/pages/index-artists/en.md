This is the way into the artists on AfricanArtists.com: more than a hundred profiles of painters, sculptors and photographers from across Africa and its diaspora, from the 1920s to today, and lists of nearly nine hundred more by country, by medium and by generation. Start with the lists below, browse the profiles, or read the short introduction to some of the names that matter most.

The profiles span the continent and its languages on purpose: Anglophone West and East Africa, Francophone Mali, Senegal and the Congo, Lusophone Mozambique, Arabic-speaking Egypt, Sudan, Algeria and Morocco, and Southern Africa. For the schools these artists formed or rebelled against, see [art movements](/movements); for women artists, see our guide to [African women artists](/guides/african-women-artists).

## Pioneers and the schools

**[Mahmoud Mokhtar](/artists/mahmoud-mokhtar)** (Egypt, 1891–1934) made *Egypt's Awakening*, a monumental sculpture in Cairo that became a national emblem of the 1920s.

**Gerard Sekoto** (South Africa, 1913–1993) painted township life, left for Paris in 1947 and became a founding figure of Black South African modernism.

**[Inji Efflatoun](/artists/inji-aflatoun)** (Egypt, 1924–1989) painted her fellow prisoners from her own cell, joining art, politics and feminism. See [African women artists](/guides/african-women-artists).

**[Chaïbia Talal](/artists/chaibia-talal)** (Morocco, 1929–2004) was a self-taught painter of vivid, spontaneous canvases who belonged to no school, a contrast with the [Casablanca School](/movements/casablanca-school) around her.

**Mohamed Melehi** (Morocco) helped found the Casablanca School with [Farid Belkahia](/artists/farid-belkahia), and is known for bold, rhythmic wave patterns in strong colour.

**[Uche Okeke](/artists/uche-okeke)** (Nigeria) helped shape the [Zaria Art Society](/movements/zaria-art-society) and its idea of "natural synthesis", the argument that modern Nigerian art should draw on its own traditions.

**[Bruce Onobrakpeya](/artists/bruce-onobrakpeya)** (Nigeria) is a printmaker and painter, also linked to the Zaria Art Society, whose work draws on the textures and stories of Nigeria's many peoples.

**Bodys Isek Kingelez** (DR Congo, 1948–2015) built fantastical model cities from paper, cardboard and found materials, imagining a Kinshasa of the future.

## Sculptors and installation artists

**[Romuald Hazoumè](/artists/romuald-hazoume)** (Benin) makes masks from discarded petrol cans, many of them referring to fuel smuggling and to the Atlantic slave trade. See [African masks](/art-forms/african-masks).

**[Yinka Shonibare MBE](/artists/yinka-shonibare)** (Nigeria and UK) uses Dutch wax print, a fabric sold across Africa as "African" but made in Europe, to dress headless figures in Victorian costume.

**Serge Attukwei Clottey** (Ghana) turns yellow plastic jerrycans into woven wall pieces and sculptures, a practice he calls Afrogallonism.

**[Ibrahim Mahama](/artists/ibrahim-mahama)** (Ghana) wraps buildings, monuments and railway wagons in jute sacks, tracing the trade that moves through them. See [African textiles](/art-forms/african-textiles).

**Gonçalo Mabunda** (Mozambique) makes thrones, masks and sculptures from decommissioned weapons left over from the civil war. See [African sculpture](/art-forms/african-sculpture).

**Nicholas Hlobo** (South Africa) stitches rubber inner tubes and laces them with ribbon, drawing on Xhosa ceremony and on questions of identity.

**Meschac Gaba** (Benin) is best known for his *Museum of Contemporary African Art*, an installation of rooms built from cloth, games and everyday objects.

**Abdoulaye Konaté** (Mali) makes large textile works woven from strips of cloth, often addressing social and political questions. See [African textiles](/art-forms/african-textiles).

**Barthélémy Toguo** (Cameroon) makes installations, drawings and prints about migration, globalisation and the body, working between Cameroon and Europe.

## Painters

**[William Kentridge](/artists/william-kentridge)** (South Africa) draws in charcoal, erases and redraws each image, and films the results, from *Felix in Exile* onward. See [contemporary African art](/guides/contemporary-african-art).

**[Marlene Dumas](/artists/marlene-dumas)** (South Africa, based in Amsterdam) paints portraits from photographs, with a haunting, unfinished intimacy.

**Julie Mehretu** (Ethiopia, based in the United States) builds large, layered drawings and paintings of cities, maps and architecture.

**Michael Armitage** (Kenya) paints large canvases that layer Kenyan folklore, history and colonial-era imagery.

**[Amoako Boafo](/artists/amoako-boafo)** (Ghana) paints portraits of friends and strangers in bold colour and expressive, sculptural brushwork.

## Photographers and visual artists

**[Samuel Fosso](/artists/samuel-fosso)** (Cameroon) makes staged self-portraits in costume that play with identity, fashion and stereotype. See [African photography](/art-forms/african-photography).

**Lalla Essaydi** (Morocco) photographs women in embroidered fabric covered in henna calligraphy, challenging stereotypes of Arab women.

**[Hassan Hajjaj](/artists/hassan-hajjaj)** (Morocco and UK) frames colourful portraits with Moroccan fabrics, tins and everyday objects, linking Marrakech and London.

**[Youssef Nabil](/artists/youssef-nabil)** (Egypt) hand-tints photographic portraits in the manner of early cinema and studio portraiture.

**Sammy Baloji** (DR Congo) makes photographs and films about Katanga's mining history and colonial archives. See [African photography](/art-forms/african-photography).

**[Zanele Muholi](/artists/zanele-muholi)** (South Africa) is a visual activist whose portraits of Black LGBTQIA+ South Africans, in series such as *Faces and Phases*, have reached museums worldwide.
