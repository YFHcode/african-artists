J. H. Pierneef was born in Pretoria in 1886 to a family of Dutch origin. During the South African War his family left for the Netherlands, where he studied art, and after his return he worked in Pretoria, at first as a librarian, while painting. By the 1920s he had developed his own style: simplified, monumental landscapes built from flat planes, with clear outlines and a strong sense of design, influenced by his studies of art and architecture.

His most famous work is the series of 32 panels commissioned for the new Johannesburg railway station and painted between 1929 and 1932, showing landscapes from across South Africa. They are now in the Rupert Museum in Stellenbosch. He also worked in woodcut and linocut, and his landscapes became some of the most reproduced images in South Africa.

Pierneef's ordered landscapes, almost always without people, were embraced by Afrikaner nationalism, and are today also read critically for that vision of an empty land. He died in Pretoria in 1957.
