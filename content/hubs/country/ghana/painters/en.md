Ghanaian painting grew with the nation. Kofi Antubam and Amon Kotei worked on the symbols of the new state at independence in 1957, and the art schools at Achimota and Kumasi trained painters who looked to Ghanaian forms and life for their subjects. Painters such as Atta Kwami, known for luminous abstract grids of colour, Owusu Ankomah, who covered his figures in signs and symbols, and Betty Acquah, who painted Ghanaian women and their lives, carried the tradition forward.

Today Amoako Boafo, with his finger-painted portraits, is one of the most sought-after painters in the world, and many younger painters show in Accra and abroad. Sharon Dede Padi made paintings by printing with real leaves, including a Guinness World Record. These are Ghana's painters, most looked-up first.
