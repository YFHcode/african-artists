## Camerapix

Mohamed Amin was born in Nairobi in 1943 to a family of South Asian origin and was taking pictures for newspapers while still at school. In 1963, aged about twenty, he founded his own agency, Camerapix, in Dar es Salaam, and moved it to Nairobi a few years later. From there he covered East Africa and much of the continent for international news organisations: revolutions, coups, wars and elections, as well as wildlife and travel, which Camerapix also published in books.

## Ethiopia, 1984

In October 1984 Amin and the BBC correspondent Michael Buerk reached the famine-stricken north of Ethiopia. Amin's film of a vast camp of starving people at Korem, broadcast with Buerk's report, was seen around the world. It prompted the musician Bob Geldof to organise Band Aid, whose record "Do They Know It's Christmas?" was released that December, and the Live Aid concerts of July 1985 — one of the largest humanitarian fundraising efforts there had ever been.

## Addis Ababa, 1991

In 1991, as Ethiopia's civil war ended, Amin was badly injured by the explosion of an ammunition dump in Addis Ababa; his sound recordist was killed. Amin lost his left arm. He was fitted with a prosthetic arm and went back to work, still filming.

## Flight 961

On 23 November 1996 Amin was on board Ethiopian Airlines Flight 961 when it was hijacked. The aircraft ran out of fuel and came down in the sea off the Comoros; most of the people on board were killed, Amin among them. Survivors said he had tried to reason with the hijackers.

## Legacy

Amin's archive of photographs and film is one of the largest records of post-independence East Africa. A younger photographer from Nairobi, [Dan Eldon](/artists/dan-eldon), was killed covering Somalia for Reuters in 1993.
