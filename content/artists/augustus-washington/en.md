Augustus Washington was born in 1820 in Trenton, New Jersey, the son of a formerly enslaved father. He studied at Dartmouth College and, to pay for his education, learned the new technique of the daguerreotype. In 1846 he opened a studio in Hartford, Connecticut, advertising in anti-slavery newspapers. Around 1846–47 he photographed John Brown, the abolitionist who would lead the raid on Harpers Ferry in 1859, one hand raised as if taking an oath — the earliest known portrait of him.

Disillusioned by racism in the United States, Washington emigrated in 1853 to Liberia, the West African republic founded by formerly enslaved Americans. He continued to make daguerreotypes there and in neighbouring countries, portraying Liberia's leading citizens and politicians, before turning to farming, publishing and politics. He served in Liberia's legislature, as Speaker of the House of Representatives and later as a senator.
